Foundations: power categories, domains, and AFC

Start by separating three foundations that shape every 6 GHz design decision: power category, regulatory domain, and automated frequency coordination. Power sets your maximum EIRP and power spectral density, which determine coverage reach and interference risk. Domain rules define allowed channels, indoor boundaries, and whether AFC applies to standard power devices. AFC, where required, brokers access so incumbents remain protected as you operate. For example, indoor low power has no AFC but stricter limits, while standard power typically needs AFC before transmitting. The tradeoff is straightforward: more power unlocks range and outdoor options but requires coordination and tighter controls. Map these elements early because they constrain bandwidth choices and antenna strategy, preventing late redesigns when equipment arrives or clients expose range and roaming gaps.

Define terms once to avoid confusion when comparing specifications. EIRP represents total radiated power including antenna gain, while PSD spreads that power per MHz across your channel width. Wider channels raise throughput but tighten PSD ceilings, so your configured transmit power may be reduced automatically by controllers. Domains such as FCC, Ofcom, and EU set different limits and indoor rules, which influence roaming design and channel reuse. A retail chain that spans geographies must normalize templates to the strictest region, or customize per site with careful documentation. The main risk is misapplying a single-country guide across all locations, creating silent noncompliance. Precise terminology lets teams validate numbers consistently, align procurement, and avoid disputes during auditing or incident analysis.

Turn the foundations into a practical framing sequence before tool selection. First, confirm site classification as indoor, outdoor, or mixed because that narrows the viable power categories. Next, identify the domain that governs the site, including any recent 6 GHz rulings or national exceptions. Finally, decide whether AFC is required and how you will register access points, monitor grants, and react to changes. Frequently asked questions

Do all 6 GHz deployments require AFC?

No. Standard power typically requires AFC, while low power indoor and very low power do not in many regions. Always check the governing domain because rules can vary by country and can be updated over time.

How should I choose channel width at 6 GHz?

Start from client needs and density, then confirm PSD limits per width. Wider channels increase throughput but may reduce allowed transmit power. Validate coverage and roaming performance in a pilot before applying templates broadly.

What documentation is essential for AFC registration?

Collect device identifiers, antenna gains, installation coordinates, and site classifications. Keep a reproducible dataset that matches controller templates. This supports accurate grants, easier audits, and quicker troubleshooting during changes.

Can I reuse the same template across different regulatory domains?

It is safer to maintain region specific templates or well guarded overrides. Limits differ across domains, and a single global template risks silent noncompliance. If reusing, bind limits to the strictest region and document exceptions.

What is the risk of ignoring PSD calculations?

You may overestimate coverage and performance, because controllers reduce power to meet PSD limits on wider channels. This can produce roaming gaps and retransmissions. Always compute PSD alongside EIRP during design and validation.
