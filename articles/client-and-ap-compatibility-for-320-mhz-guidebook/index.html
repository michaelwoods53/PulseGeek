Client and AP Compatibility for 320 MHz

By Rachel Morgan • Published January 15, 2026

Choosing devices for 320 MHz requires understanding client behavior, AP capabilities, and compatibility boundaries. This guide orients practitioners who plan WiFi 8 rollouts across laptops, phones, and routers, and want predictable outcomes. You will get a framework for verifying support, mapping tradeoffs, and sequencing tests before production changes.

Key takeaways

  • Verify 320 MHz support by chipset, driver version, and operating system.

Frequently asked questions

Do all WiFi 8 devices support 320 MHz?

No. Support depends on chipset capability, drivers, and OS exposure. Many devices negotiate 6 GHz but only up to 160 MHz. Verify by model and software build, then test association and sustained throughput under consistent conditions.

Is 320 MHz always faster than 160 MHz?

Not always. Wider channels require stronger SNR to hold high MCS. If noise or reflections reduce modulation, 160 MHz can outperform 320 MHz. Measure at fixed distances and switch widths to see which delivers steadier rates.

Should I enable 320 MHz on every SSID?

Start with a single SSID on 6 GHz for validation. Keep a known good lane on 80 or 160 MHz for reliability. Expand only after clients associate cleanly and maintain stable rates in your target rooms.

Will WiFi 7 clients use 320 MHz on a WiFi 8 AP?

Typically no. Most WiFi 7 clients do not support 320 MHz. Some may attempt wider bonding but show instability at range. Plan mixed estates around 160 MHz until you confirm device behavior through repeatable tests.

How do mixed client tiers affect airtime?

High tier laptops can dominate airtime if scheduling is not tuned. Monitor negotiated rates and retries, and consider policies that cap aggressive clients. Where needed, keep a narrower SSID to protect voice and latency sensitive traffic.
