CRT Overlay Packs for Miyoo Mini Plus: Top Picks

By Jared Butler • Published January 6, 2026

Picking CRT overlays for Miyoo Mini Plus starts with respecting small screens, gentle scanlines, and the device’s limited headroom. I evaluated overlays that rely on simple PNG masks and sane opacity, then tested scale behavior with integer settings to avoid shimmer. The goal is a clean CRT feel without heavy shader costs. You will find overlay packs that pair well with RetroArch on Miyoo, plus guidance on aspect ratio choices and mild bezel art. Where shader-heavy looks are tempting, I call out the performance tradeoff and offer lighter substitutes. If you are new to UI adjustments, consider skimming a practical overview of UI tweaks and overlays on Miyoo for context before diving into these picks.

Key takeaways

  • Favor PNG-based CRT masks with modest opacity to preserve performance.
  • Use integer scaling when possible to reduce shimmer and moiré artifacts.
  • Prefer per-system overlays sized to native aspect for sharper results.
  • Test overlays in a dark scene and a bright scene before deciding.
  • Keep one lightweight preset ready for demanding cores or large bezels.

1) Subtle Scanline PNG Pack

Choose a subtle scanline PNG set when you want a CRT flavor without distracting patterns. Lightweight 1080p or 720p PNG masks scaled down can look convincing on Miyoo Mini Plus if opacity stays around 10 to 20 percent and lines align with integer scaling. For example, a thin even-line mask paired with 4x or 5x vertical integer scale often avoids shimmer on scrolling backgrounds in 16-bit platformers. The tradeoff is reduced authenticity compared to complex shadow masks, since single-pass PNGs cannot emulate phosphor triads or beam bloom. However, the simplicity keeps input latency predictable and avoids battery drain spikes that appear when stacking heavier shaders. If moiré appears, nudge opacity lower or switch to a variant with slightly thicker lines to better match the handheld’s pixel grid.

Section highlights

  • Use 10 to 20 percent opacity and integer scaling for stability.
  • Test scrolling scenes and adjust line thickness to reduce shimmer.

2) PVM-Inspired Static Mask

A PVM-inspired static mask overlay delivers a tighter, professional CRT look using a fine grid that enhances perceived sharpness on pixel art. A minimal PNG pattern with tiny square apertures can make SNES or Genesis tiles pop while keeping text readable on the Mini Plus display. For instance, pairing a light grid with 4x scale often yields crisp UI elements without smearing. The limitation is that static grids can reveal interference on diagonal motion if the pattern fights the source resolution. Mitigate this by testing two mask densities and picking the one that lands closest to integer alignment.   • Fine grid masks boost sharpness when paired with integer scaling.
  • Keep two densities ready to avoid interference on diagonal motion.

3) System-Themed Thin Bezels

System-themed thin bezels add context without stealing screen real estate, which matters on a small handheld. A narrow border that echoes SNES or Mega Drive styling can hide pillarbox bars and visually center the playfield. For example, a 2 to 4 percent border width gives room for a discrete logo while keeping the active area large enough for RPG text. The drawback is cumulative cost when pairing decorative bezels with scanline masks and any shader. On the Mini Plus, pick either a thin bezel plus mask, or a bezel alone, to avoid compounding overhead. 4) Game Boy Mono LCD Overlay

A monochrome LCD overlay tailored for Game Boy restores the grid and slight vignette that define that display type while keeping sprites crisp. A gentle dot matrix PNG with 12 to 18 percent opacity, combined with a warm tint, evokes classic handhelds without introducing blur. Try it with integer scaling and a square pixel aspect to keep text clean in RPGs and puzzle titles. The tradeoff is reduced brightness under strong ambient light because the overlay darkens mids. To counter this, keep a brighter variant with 8 to 10 percent opacity as a second preset for outdoor play. If you notice notifications blocking the minimal top border, consider tidying on-screen notifications and HUD so overlay edges remain unobstructed during saves and state loads.

Rounded corner overlays with a faint vignette suggest CRT curvature without heavy shader math. A simple PNG that darkens the extreme corners by 5 to 12 percent can frame the scene and hide minor scaling mismatches. For example, platformers benefit from a gentle edge falloff that keeps eyes inside the action zone. The limitation is potential detail loss in HUD elements that sit near the corners, such as timer digits or life counters. Test HUD-heavy titles to ensure legibility before committing. 6) Scanline Plus Light Shadow Mask

Combining a thin scanline PNG with a very light shadow mask can add depth for 16-bit sprites while remaining efficient. Use two layers with conservative opacity, such as 12 percent lines and 6 percent mask, to avoid moiré. This works well in action titles where rich colors benefit from a hint of subpixel texture. The compromise is added setup complexity and greater risk of pattern interference at non-integer scales. Keep integer scaling on when possible and store two presets for 4x and 5x vertical scales. Looking ahead

The next step is to normalize your testing path so decisions stick. Build a short loop that loads the same three scenes for each system, one dark, one bright, and one with diagonal motion. On Miyoo Mini Plus, this routine exposes scanline shimmer, corner vignettes that hide UI, and color tints that crush midtones. Save two presets per system, one conservative and one richer, labeled with opacity and scale notes. This reduces guesswork weeks later when you forget why a choice was made. If you prefer guardrails, keep a fallback preset with no overlay so you can immediately feel the marginal cost when a pack becomes too busy or introduces subtle blur you did not notice during initial testing.

Performance should remain predictable while you experiment, and that depends as much on core and save settings as it does on overlays. If loading states during A and B comparisons stutter or crackle audio, you will misjudge masks and be tempted to overcorrect. Before deep visual tuning, review a comprehensive RetroArch Miyoo setup that emphasizes stable storage, sensible autosave cadence, and latency controls. With that baseline, changes you attribute to art or scaling really are caused by those choices. Spread overlay adjustments across different games and genres. A palette that flatters platformers might dull arcade shooters. Keep notes per genre and be willing to split presets accordingly.

As your library grows, favor a sustainable folder scheme and naming that reflects aspect and opacity. Without structure, you will re-test the same combinations and create conflicting overrides that undo your best work. A clean approach looks like grouping overlays by system with subfolders for masks and bezels, then per-content overrides that point to the correct pair. Add a short README in each system folder listing tested scale integers and any edge cases like titles that place UI near corners. This discipline pays off later when you migrate to another Miyoo model or a different handheld. You will have a portable baseline that travels with minimal friction.

Frequently asked questions

Do overlays slow down Miyoo Mini Plus?

PNG-based overlays have minimal cost compared to multi-pass shaders. Stacking multiple images or pairing with heavy shaders can add overhead. Start with one overlay and test a demanding scene before saving changes.

Should I use integer scaling with CRT overlays?

Yes when possible. Integer scaling aligns the overlay pattern with the game’s pixel grid, which reduces shimmer and moiré. If alignment is impossible on a title, lower overlay opacity to minimize interference.

Where should overlay files live on Miyoo RetroArch?

Place PNGs in the overlays directory and keep per-system subfolders. Save configuration files alongside the images or in a configs folder. Use per-content overrides so different systems reference the correct files.

How bright should scanlines be on a small screen?

Start light. A range near 8 to 20 percent opacity usually preserves clarity on small displays. Increase only if text remains readable and no shimmer appears during scrolling or diagonal motion.

Can I combine bezels with scanlines safely?

Yes but test the impact. A thin bezel plus a light scanline mask is often fine. Avoid adding complex shaders on top. If input feels less responsive or battery drops faster, simplify to a single overlay.
