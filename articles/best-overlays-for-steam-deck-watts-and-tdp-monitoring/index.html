Best Overlays for Steam Deck Watts and TDP Monitoring

By Tyler Brooks • Published February 17, 2026

Only a handful of overlays on Steam Deck give trustworthy visibility into watts, TDP, and frame timing without distracting from play. This list focuses on tools that install cleanly, expose useful power signals, and help you confirm whether a change actually improves efficiency. I favored overlays that provide stable readings across games and keep configuration light. Where a tool shines, I show a quick setup and one validation pass that ties readings to in-game behavior. When a limitation matters, I call it out so you do not chase noise or misinterpret a graph. For deeper measurement practice, see this guide to monitoring battery health, cycle count, overlays, logging, temperature, and accurate playtime estimates in the related section later.

Key takeaways

  • Use SteamOS performance overlay first for consistent watts and frame time.
  • Add MangoHud when you need configurable graphs and lightweight logging.
  • Turn to PowerTools to tune TDP quickly, then validate with gameplay.
  • Cross check readings during steady scenes to reduce variability noise.
  • Anchor decisions to comfort, stability, and battery drain, not single metrics.

1) SteamOS performance overlay

The built in SteamOS performance overlay is the most consistent starting point because it pairs directly with the system’s power manager and Gamescope. At higher levels, it shows frame time graphs and a battery drain estimate that maps well to observed session length when scenes are steady. A quick test is to enable the overlay at level three, lock an FPS cap, and repeat a 60 second hallway walk in a game’s hub area to stabilize load. This reveals whether a TDP change actually reduces watts or just shifts heat and fan speed. The tradeoff is limited customization and sparse labeling, so you must learn how each overlay level groups metrics. It also lacks file logging, which means you should complement it with a tool capable of exporting data if you plan comparisons across games.

Use this overlay as your baseline whenever changing TDP in tools like PowerTools to avoid confounding factors. For instance, cap at 40 FPS, set a modest TDP like 10 watts, and observe the frame time band settle. If bands widen or stutter appears while battery drain does not drop, the cap is starving the GPU rather than saving energy. The why matters here. Frame stability drives perceived smoothness more than raw FPS once your ceiling is stable. A limitation is that the battery estimate is an average over recent draw and can lag after scene changes. Take readings after a short dwell, and log observations in a simple note so you can revisit them alongside a more detailed overlay later.

  • Check the overlay at a consistent spot to reduce scene variability.
  • Prefer higher overlay levels when testing caps and frame pacing.

2) MangoHud configurable OSD

MangoHud is the most flexible overlay for Steam Deck when you want specific metrics like battery percentage, CPU and GPU temperature, and frame time with a lightweight footprint. The claim to prefer MangoHud emerges when you need quick file logging or a clearer frametime plot than the built in options. A concrete setup is to enable MangoHud per game, show FPS, frametime, and battery, then compare two TDP values over matched scenes. Read frame time variance next to battery trend rather than FPS alone. The tradeoff is learning its options and ensuring environment variables are set correctly inside Steam. Because different render paths expose sensors differently, some metrics may not appear in every title. Keep your config minimal first, then add just the metrics that inform a decision you are testing.

To streamline per game testing, inject MangoHud through Launch Options so you can toggle metrics without leaving SteamOS. This keeps your workflow tight as you iterate on caps and TDP. If you want to go deeper on validating measurements across sessions, consider exporting aggregated statistics and comparing them with guides on logging power draw by game over time. You can also revisit a comprehensive battery optimization plan to decide when to cap FPS, adjust brightness, or disable Wi Fi for the best return. These companion practices round out what MangoHud shows on screen and help turn screen overlays into durable settings you do not need to revisit every week.

Here is a compact Launch Options example that enables MangoHud and sets a readable metric set. You should see FPS, frame time, temperatures, and battery at the top of your screen. Try the same hallway walk for sixty seconds at two TDP values and compare average frame time along with battery percentage change. If numbers do not move in the direction expected, your scene may be CPU limited or background tasks are skewing results.

3) Decky PowerTools for fast TDP tuning

Decky PowerTools is the quickest way to adjust TDP and clocks on Steam Deck while keeping your eyes on a live overlay. The claim to use it comes from speed. You can lower TDP in small steps during gameplay, then immediately glance at frame time and battery draw using the SteamOS or MangoHud overlay discussed earlier. A practical sequence is to cap at a target FPS, drop TDP by one watt, and repeat until either stutter appears or battery draw plateaus. This makes TDP a scalpel instead of a blunt knob. The tradeoff is that PowerTools is a plugin and relies on Decky Loader. Plugin updates can lag behind SteamOS changes, so always validate that the TDP limit you set is respected by confirming performance effects rather than trusting the slider alone.

Pairing PowerTools with a monitoring overlay prevents overcorrection. If frame time spikes after a small TDP decrease but battery readings barely fall, the bottleneck moved to the CPU and overall efficiency suffered. In this case, raise TDP by one notch and hold a steady scene to retest. Looking ahead

A repeatable workflow turns overlays into durable settings. Start by choosing one monitoring view as your ground truth and stick with it while tuning TDP or FPS. The best baseline for many players is the built in performance overlay because it behaves predictably across titles. Then layer MangoHud only when you need additional metrics or a clearer frame time view. This reduces context switching and helps you judge changes consistently. The tradeoff is fewer bells and whistles at first, but clarity will save you time. As you get comfortable, expand your overlay slowly and add one metric at a time. This guards against decision fatigue from too many numbers that do not move the outcome you care about.

Next, link your overlay work to a short validation pass. Use a fixed camera walk or a training room and run at least two sixty second trials for each setting. Record the average frame time and the direction of battery trend rather than a single snapshot. If you want structured comparisons, learn how to log Steam Deck power draw by game over time using reliable tools to capture repeatable data. When readings disagree, trust gameplay feel first and investigate whether background services or thermal limits changed the context. Overlays are instruments, and instruments gain meaning when paired with a test plan.

Finally, set thresholds that trigger a change. For example, if your frame time variance exceeds a small band and the battery trend does not improve by a noticeable margin, revert the last TDP or cap change. If heat or fan noise becomes intrusive, prefer a slightly lower cap matched with a stable TDP rather than pushing clocks. To keep your deck healthy, review safe operating temperature ranges and confirm your results against a guide that monitors battery health and cycle count. These habits make watts and TDP readings actionable and keep your play sessions both smooth and efficient.

Frequently asked questions

Which overlay should I start with for reliable watts?

Begin with the SteamOS performance overlay because it is tightly integrated and stable across games. Use it to baseline changes, then add MangoHud only when you need extra metrics or logging.

How do I know a TDP change is actually helping?

Hold a steady scene, cap FPS, and compare average frame time alongside battery trend. A helpful change reduces drain without widening frame time variance or introducing stutter.

Do I need logging or is on screen data enough?

On screen data is fine for quick checks. Use logging when comparing settings across sessions or games, where small differences can hide behind scene variability.

Are overlay watts exact measurements?

No. They are estimates based on system sensors and short term averages. Expect lag after scene changes and confirm trends over a minute rather than a single moment.
