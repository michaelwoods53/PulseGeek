Steam Deck vs Legion Go Battery Life Tests

By Tyler Brooks • Published January 13, 2026

Shoppers weighing Steam Deck against Lenovo Legion Go often ask how battery life differs when settings are matched. We test both with frame rate caps, brightness targets, and comparable profiles to show practical playtime, not lab peak numbers. The lens is stability over raw wattage, because travel and couch use demand predictable hours. You will see how display choices, TDP limits, and game type shift drain, and why the fairest baseline matters for any head-to-head battery comparison.

Key takeaways

  • Match brightness, frame caps, and TDP before judging battery differences.
  • Game complexity drives watts more than device branding or hype.
  • Use 30 or 40 fps caps to stabilize battery and thermals.
  • High refresh displays hurt runtime unless downclocked and dimmed.
  • For travel, plan per hour watt needs against your charger capacity.

Evaluation criteria and how to weigh them

Fair testing starts by controlling brightness, because screen power can skew results more than any single silicon tweak. Use a shared luminance target, such as a measured mid level that remains legible indoors, then lock both devices near that nit value. A practical rule is selecting the lowest comfortable brightness that still shows UI and HUD cleanly. The tradeoff is outdoor visibility may force higher levels, but documenting that context explains any runtime shift. Without a brightness baseline, Steam and Legion comparisons quickly confuse panel efficiency with GPU budget, which misleads buyers deciding between handhelds.

Frame rate caps shape average power draw predictably, so weight them more than headline specs. A 30 or 40 fps cap often trims CPU and GPU scheduling overhead while preserving smooth input, especially with VRR or good frame pacing. For action titles, 40 can balance motion clarity with hours, while indie games may thrive at 30 with minimal input penalty. The limitation is genres that depend on high refresh will feel compromised, yet the upside is steadier thermals and longer battery. Consider caps as the first lever, because they remain consistent across Steam Deck and Legion Go configurations.

TDP limits translate intent into watts, but they interact with game workload and API choices, so weight them with nuance. Setting a conservative power ceiling can prevent runaway boosts and fan spikes, making battery outcomes more reproducible in travel use. As an example, pinning a mid TDP with a 40 fps cap keeps frame times stable and preserves hour-to-hour predictability. The tradeoff is potential artifacting in intensive scenes where the cap is missed, but the benefit is measured battery planning. When comparing across devices, publish the exact TDP values and note if per-title variations were required to maintain target frames.

Frequently asked questions

How do you make brightness fair between devices?

Use a shared luminance target measured with a meter or a repeatable mid setting that is comfortably readable indoors. Keep that target constant during tests and document any deviations when lighting conditions force changes.

Is 40 fps more efficient than 60 fps on handhelds?

Often yes, because a 40 fps cap lowers scheduling overhead and reduces boost behavior while preserving motion smoothness. The exact savings depend on the game workload and API, but caps usually stabilize power and thermals.

Should I limit TDP or rely on battery saver modes?

Both work, but a manual TDP limit combined with a frame cap provides more predictable results. Saver modes are quick and broad, while TDP and fps caps let you target your genre and comfort preferences with better repeatability.

Do high refresh displays always reduce battery life?

Not always, but higher refresh can add overhead even when frame rates are capped. Lowering refresh to 60 and using a 30 or 40 fps cap typically reduces draw. Visual smoothness may drop slightly compared to full refresh.

What charger spec is enough for stable capped play?

A 45 to 65 watt USB PD charger usually provides headroom for capped play and charging. Confirm the cable supports the negotiated power level and check that your settings prevent unnecessary boost behavior during plugged sessions.
