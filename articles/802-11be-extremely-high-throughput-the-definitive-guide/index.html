Spectrum and channelization strategy

Channelization choices decide whether EHT headroom becomes real throughput or collapses under collisions. Wider channels improve peak rates when the noise profile is clean, yet they amplify the cost of partial interference. Puncturing allows you to exclude dirty sub‑blocks so more of the channel remains usable, but misjudged patterns can strand bandwidth. A practical approach is to begin with the narrowest width that meets capacity under expected concurrency, then widen with measured margin. That protects stability while you learn the site’s interference rhythms. When you widen, test the exact puncturing pattern you plan to deploy, because adjacent systems may not share the same assumptions.

Coexistence in 6 GHz offers cleaner spectrum, yet rules and allocations shape feasibility by region. Indoor low power limits keep EIRP modest, which contains interference but reduces reach, so density planning must account for smaller cells. In mixed bands, you also balance DFS constraints and legacy operation that can steal airtime at the edges. The safest habit is to reserve a protected 6 GHz SSID for modern clients while maintaining compatible service elsewhere. This segmentation keeps new features from being dragged into legacy contention. It also surfaces usable differences during captures and performance reviews, which helps you correct design errors early.

The risk of copying another site’s plan is that their noise floor and building materials differ. Document your assumptions and the telemetry that supports them so reviews are reproducible. Revisit choices after meaningful changes, like tenant churn or new adjacent systems, to avoid slow degradation that hides behind averages.

Migration planning and readiness

Client readiness determines how much of the feature set you can safely enable. Map firmware versions and driver requirements to the minimum needed for stable EHT negotiation. Performance testing and validation

Build repeatable EHT throughput testing steps, tools, and controls for valid results, then hold testers to those procedures. Measure latency and throughput together so you catch pathological tails that averages hide. Frequently asked questions

When should I widen to 320 MHz channels?

Widen only when spectrum is consistently clean and backhaul plus client SNR can support it. Validate puncturing patterns under real interference and confirm latency tails remain within your success range. If either fails, hold a narrower width.

Do I need multi link operation enabled on day one?

No. Start with single link stability, then pilot multi link for a subset of modern clients. Keep revert plans and measure both throughput and jitter. Expand after a week of routine use without anomalies or excessive support tickets.

How do I prioritize security settings for 6 GHz?

Lead with strong authentication, PMF, and segmentation for 6 GHz SSIDs. Validate onboarding and policy paths for modern devices, then layer optional performance features. Review these settings quarterly to prevent configuration drift as firmware evolves.

What metrics prove an EHT migration is ready to scale?

Use pass ranges for throughput and 95th percentile latency under expected concurrency. Confirm retry rates and roaming stability stay within thresholds during busy hours. Require backhaul checks and client driver versions before expanding beyond pilot sites.

Why do lab results differ from field performance?

Labs control noise and mobility that field environments cannot. Building materials, adjacent systems, and human patterns shift interference and SNR. Favor field corrections that protect stability, document changes, and retest to confirm risk reduction before wider rollout.
