PS1 on Miyoo Mini: Performance Tips That Work

By Jeremy Crawford • Published January 12, 2026

These performance tips target PS1 on the Miyoo Mini using clear, low-risk adjustments that protect stability. We prioritized settings that improve frame pacing and audio without heavy tradeoffs, then added selective per-game overrides for edge cases. Items were chosen based on behavior common to PS1 emulation, how RetroArch surfaces options on this handheld, and what changes produce consistent gains with minimal regressions. You will see advice on core choice, frameskip and sync, audio latency, and clean override usage. When a decision has real cost, we explain the tradeoffs so you can judge whether the benefit fits your play style.

Key takeaways

  • Prefer PCSX ReARMed with dynarec on and visual enhancements off.
  • Use frameskip sparingly and favor consistent frame pacing over peaks.
  • Raise audio latency a little to remove crackle during heavy scenes.
  • Apply per-game overrides only when a title truly needs them.
  • Record changes and revert fast if stability regresses after tweaks.

1) PCSX ReARMed, dynarec, and clean visuals

Start with PCSX ReARMed and enable the dynamic recompiler, because the Miyoo Mini benefits most from CPU-side gains rather than visual extras. On this device, leave enhanced resolution, texture filtering, and PGXP-style features off, since they add overhead that the screen cannot fully showcase. For example, Ridge Racer runs more consistently when the recompiler is on and filtering is disabled, delivering steadier frame pacing and fewer hitching moments. The tradeoff is that you will not get the sharper polygons seen on stronger hardware, yet the payoff is stable play and lower input delay. This balance respects the handheld’s limits while preserving the look that matches original hardware output on a small display.

A practical rule is to prefer native internal resolution with dithering kept as-is or managed by the core’s safe default, because aggressive tweaks often introduce uneven load and audio crackle. Chrono Cross, for instance, benefits from native visuals plus dynarec on, where enabling texture smoothing can tilt scenes into brief slowdowns during effects. The limitation is that some titles with heavy geometry may still dip during peak effects, but keeping visuals simple preserves headroom for the worst scenes. If you need a broader baseline by system, review per-system guidance in the article on per-system emulator settings for Miyoo Mini and use PS1 notes as your template.

2) Frameskip and sync for smooth play

Use frameskip as a last resort and keep it low, because steady pacing beats occasional peaks followed by stutter. Setting frameskip to 0 achieves full visual fidelity when the scene allows it, while a cautious value of 1 can mask rare dips in effects-heavy moments. As an example, Tekken 3 feels better with 0 frameskip plus vsync on, but if brief drops bother you, a temporary frameskip 1 can smooth timing at the cost of frame delivery. The tradeoff is lost animation frames and a slightly choppier look, yet it prevents audio crackle and input jitter. Be wary of high frameskip values that undermine responsiveness, especially in fighters and rhythm-heavy sequences where cadence matters.

Sync choices can stabilize output without pushing the CPU harder. Enabling vsync often improves consistency, and using a frame delay that the Mini can handle helps tighten input without provoking stutter. Silent Hills’ fog scenes, for instance, can stutter if you push frame delay too far toward zero; easing back avoids buffer underruns. A good method is to adjust one timing control at a time and test a tough scene for thirty to sixty seconds before keeping the change. If you want broader timing guidance beyond PS1, compare the frameskip guidance for Miyoo Mini to understand when small skips outpace raw rendering tweaks.

  • Adjust vsync and frame delay slowly, testing difficult segments.

3) Audio latency and resampler tuning

Increasing audio latency a little is a reliable way to remove crackle during PS1 peaks on the Miyoo Mini. Start by nudging the audio buffer up in small steps until heavy effects stop popping, then leave it there to preserve headroom. For example, Metal Gear Solid codec scenes can spike load; raising the buffer removes stutter without affecting scene timing. The tradeoff is a bit more audio delay that very sensitive players might notice, yet the benefit is a consistently clean soundtrack that hides minor frame turbulence. Keep the resampler simple and avoid high-quality modes that cost CPU, since the handheld’s small speaker or headphones will not expose those improvements as much as stability does.

Test audio changes alongside your frameskip and sync decisions because they interact. A too-aggressive frame delay can starve the audio buffer, and a tiny buffer can exaggerate micro stutters. Pick a challenging room or battle and loop it for a minute with a stopwatch to confirm the crackle is gone. If you need broader RetroArch audio context beyond PS1 specifics, the focused walkthrough on reducing audio latency on Miyoo Mini shows where buffers, drivers, and synchronization meet. The limitation is that some games with unusual audio mixing can still hiccup briefly, but a modest buffer and conservative sync will keep artifacts rare and short-lived.

4) Per-game overrides with a light touch

Per-game overrides solve outliers without burdening everything else, so save them for titles that misbehave. In RetroArch, create a Game Override when one PS1 game stutters or needs a unique option, such as a small frameskip or a slightly higher audio buffer. For example, if Vagrant Story’s busy rooms dip below full speed, set frameskip 1 as a per-game change instead of raising it globally. The upside is a clean global baseline that stays fast, and the cost is extra setup time and a bit more configuration to track. Keep notes on what you changed and why so rollback is easy. Frequently asked questions

Which PS1 core should I use on Miyoo Mini?

Use PCSX ReARMed with the dynamic recompiler enabled. It delivers the best balance of speed and compatibility for this device. Keep enhancement features off to conserve headroom and maintain stable input timing.

Is frameskip safe to set globally for PS1?

Keep frameskip at 0 globally for full animation. If one game dips, apply frameskip 1 as a per-game override. Higher values reduce responsiveness and can harm timing in fighters or rhythm scenes.

How do I remove audio crackle without hurting performance?

Increase the audio buffer in small steps until crackle vanishes during the heaviest scenes. Avoid high-cost resampler modes. Re-test a demanding segment to confirm the change holds under load.

Do visual enhancements improve PS1 clarity on this screen?

Not meaningfully. The handheld’s display and CPU budget favor native resolution with simple filtering. Enhancements often add overhead that causes stutter without delivering noticeable clarity at this size.

Looking ahead

Build a stable global baseline, then adjust only where a game truly stumbles. That rhythm keeps PS1 on the Miyoo Mini predictable and easy to maintain. As you test more titles, record small notes per game and revisit older changes with fresh eyes. If a tweak no longer helps, remove it and simplify. When you move beyond PS1, apply the same method to other systems by isolating one variable at a time and testing a challenging scene for a short, repeatable window. That habit yields dependable results that survive firmware updates and new cores without surprises.
