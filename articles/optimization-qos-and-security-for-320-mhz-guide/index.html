Pitfalls and edge cases

Overextending cell size is the most common 320 MHz failure mode. Designers accept a lower edge SNR for the promise of peak rates, then voice collapses during handoffs. Mitigate by capping transmit power and measuring roaming thresholds where devices actually decide to move. If reuse is poor, consider stepping down to 160 MHz in select areas while keeping 320 MHz where line of sight dominates. Before making that change, revisit assess whether 320 MHz adds value by role and site profile so changes reflect business priorities rather than blanket settings. Use test calls that wander the cell edge to verify jitter holds within acceptable bounds.

Another trap is assuming outdoor operation mirrors indoor patterns. At 6 GHz, Automated Frequency Coordination imposes constraints that shape channel availability and power across geography. For roof links or campus greens, study design outdoor 6 GHz links under AFC constraints and align antenna selection and mounting to your allowed EIRP. Expect obstructions and foliage to increase loss compared with indoor paths. Counter with careful alignment and acceptance that narrower widths may outperform under variable weather. The why is straightforward: stability beats theoretical rate when links face dynamic attenuation outside controlled buildings.

Small organizations can miss that fewer APs do not always equal lower cost when support calls rise. Ultra wide channels can magnify edge issues when clients vary widely. For resource constrained teams, follow the small business roadmap for adopting 320 MHz wisely to avoid overbuilding while retaining upgrade paths. This mix accepts modest throughput tradeoffs to reduce contention where people cluster and talk simultaneously.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use 320 MHz across the entire site?

Usually no. Start with open areas where SNR is strong and clients are capable, then keep narrower widths in dense rooms to improve reuse and latency predictability.

Does 320 MHz always improve voice quality?

Not by itself. Voice improves when fairness, proper QoS markings, and stable roaming are in place. Wide lanes without airtime control can increase jitter under load.

When should I enable dynamic bandwidth operation?

Consider it when interference or occupancy varies significantly. Use guardrails to avoid frequent width changes during active calls and verify latency stays within targets.

What security settings matter most for 320 MHz?

Use WPA3 Enterprise with EAP TLS, enable Protected Management Frames, and configure fast transitions. These reduce attack surface and keep roam delays low.
