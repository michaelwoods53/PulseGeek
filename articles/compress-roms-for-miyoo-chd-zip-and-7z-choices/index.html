Compress ROMs for Miyoo: CHD, ZIP, and 7z Choices

By Jared Butler • Published March 7, 2026

Choosing how to compress ROMs influences performance, battery life, and library reliability on a Miyoo handheld. This guide compares CHD for disc images, ZIP for cartridge titles, and 7z for long term storage, then walks through safe conversion steps. I focus on keeping RetroArch stable while reducing space, with examples that show how CHD helps disc games and where ZIP or 7z fits without slowing loads.

Key takeaways

  • Use CHD for disc-based games to shrink size with minimal overhead.
  • Keep cartridge ROMs in ZIP for quick loads and broad compatibility.
  • Reserve 7z for archival sets, not regular play on handheld hardware.
  • Convert with verified cuesheets to avoid track or audio mismatches.
  • Test a sample before batch processing to catch edge case issues.

1) Choose CHD for disc-based ROMs

Use CHD for CD and DVD based games because it reduces storage while maintaining accurate sector data and cue timing that many cores require. For example, converting a PS1 BIN plus CUE set to CHD often cuts size by 30 to 60 percent depending on audio tracks, yet boots quickly on cores that support compressed discs. The tradeoff is small CPU overhead at load and during streaming, which can surface on weaker cores or when audio tracks are heavily compressed. Still, the Miyoo runs many CD systems acceptably with CHD, and the format’s structure preserves subchannel and track order when converted from a correct cuesheet. If a specific core struggles, keep a handful of heavy streaming titles uncompressed, but treat that as an exception rather than the rule.

CHD also simplifies multi file discs by consolidating scattered BIN files and external audio into one container, which reduces playlist mistakes and accidental file loss. A Sega Saturn game with several tracks is a good example where CHD replaces dozens of small files with one consistent package that scans cleanly in playlists. The main limitation is conversion quality is only as good as the source cuesheet and tracks, so bad rips will carry their errors forward. Always validate the CUE references and ensure track order is correct before conversion. If a title exhibits missing music or looping glitches after changeover, retest from the original layout rather than assuming CHD itself is at fault.

  • CHD reduces disc size and keeps cue timing for supported cores.
  • Validate cuesheets first to avoid audio or track order errors.

2) Use ZIP for cartridge ROMs

ZIP is the best default for cartridge based systems because it balances modest compression with almost no overhead at load. A single SNES or Genesis ROM zipped with normal settings typically trims 5 to 20 percent and still launches rapidly through RetroArch, which matters on a handheld where cores and CPU clocks are limited. The downside is ZIP saves less space than 7z, but its broad compatibility means cores rarely object and content scanners detect titles reliably. If you manage mixed sets, ZIP keeps expected filenames, helping reconcilers match CRCs without reindexing. For everyday play, this balance beats chasing the last few megabytes. When a ROM is already tiny or compressed internally, leave it unzipped to avoid negligible gains that only complicate scraping and hashing.

ZIP also plays well with storage workflows such as incremental backups and simple SD card organization, which you might pair with consistent folder paths for saves. If your focus is reliability, keep cartridge libraries in ZIP and let RetroArch manage saves and states in their default locations. The only real drawback is that ZIP cannot match 7z on maximum compression, but the speed and support tradeoff keeps gameplay smooth on the Miyoo.

  • Use ZIP for carts and leave tiny ROMs uncompressed when gains vanish.

3) Prefer 7z only for archives

Use 7z for archival packs rather than daily play because decompression can introduce load delays and some cores struggle when containers turn complex. A large ROM set compressed with solid blocks might save more space than ZIP, yet on-device browsing slows and content scanners can time out or misreport when random access is required. For example, a full NES collection in 7z looks tidy but feels sluggish to launch repeatedly. The tradeoff is clear. You gain disk efficiency, but lose responsiveness on handheld hardware. If you maintain long term backups or off-device storage, 7z shines there with aggressive settings. Keep a playable mirror in ZIP or uncompressed form on the Miyoo and reserve 7z for the PC archive where CPU and I/O headroom make extraction painless.

Another consideration with 7z is how solid compression behaves with frequent updates. Adding or removing a few titles often triggers full recompression of the archive, which wastes time and amplifies risk if the process is interrupted. For a living library that you curate weekly, separate working sets and use lighter containers. 5) Organize mixed sets without breaking playlists

Consistent foldering and naming prevent playlist breaks when mixing CHD, ZIP, and a few uncompressed titles. Use per system directories with standardized filenames so RetroArch scanners map content reliably, and keep saves and states on the same card to reduce path confusion. A practical structure is system folders like PS1 for CHD and SNES for ZIP with a clear pattern for region tags. Frequently asked questions

Does CHD always load faster than BIN plus CUE?

No. CHD reduces size and often loads comparably, but some cores and heavy streaming scenes can show slight overhead. Test a few demanding moments before batch converting your entire library.

Should I recompress existing ZIP sets into 7z?

Only for archival storage off the device. On the handheld, 7z can slow browsing and launching. Keep a ZIP or uncompressed mirror for regular play and reserve 7z for backups on a computer.

Will converting to CHD break my existing save files?

Saves may not follow automatically because filenames change. Copy or rename save files to match the new CHD filename and test. Back up saves first in case a core treats the content as a new game.

Is there a universal compression level that works best?

No single level fits every core or game. Use CHD defaults for discs and standard ZIP for carts. If issues appear, try uncompressed for that title and document the exception in your library notes.

Can I scan mixed CHD, ZIP, and uncompressed files reliably?

Yes, if you standardize folders and filenames before scanning. Keep per system directories and avoid renaming after the scan. Rescan or rebuild playlists whenever you change structure significantly.
