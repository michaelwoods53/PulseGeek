Cisco vs Aruba: Campus WiFi Capacity Considerations

By Michael Carter • Published February 16, 2026

Cisco and Aruba dominate campus WiFi conversations because both pair deep RF controls with robust management that scales. When planning WiFi 8, capacity hinges on spectrum usage, MIMO efficiency, and backhaul readiness across buildings and floors. This comparison focuses on practical considerations that change airtime availability, especially scheduler behavior, uplink speed, and PoE headroom. We will evaluate how each vendor’s feature sets shape throughput at density, then map choices to real scenarios like lecture halls and mixed office floors. Along the way, we will point to upgrade patterns that reduce risk while maintaining service levels, so the final decision reflects performance and total cost rather than brand familiarity. The goal is not a winner, but a clear way to select the right fit for your site mix.

Key takeaways

  • Capacity depends more on RF scheduling and uplinks than peak PHY rates.
  • Cisco often benefits dense lecture halls, while Aruba favors mixed offices.
  • Multigig backhaul and PoE budgets can bottleneck WiFi performance quickly.
  • Model service levels and device mix before choosing access point families.

Evaluation criteria and how to weigh them

Capacity planning starts by defining measurable outcomes, not features, because service levels determine how much airtime you can spend per client. Prioritize consistent median throughput, acceptable 95th percentile latency, and retry rates within agreed bounds across busy hours. For WiFi 8, treat spectrum access, MIMO utilization, and backhaul headroom as primary levers, then weight management extras only if they demonstrably reduce airtime waste. A practical framing is to allocate weights like 40 percent to RF efficiency, 35 percent to wired readiness, and 25 percent to operations, adjusting for your environment. For example, a medical campus may elevate operations due to compliance audits. The risk is overvaluing peak PHY claims that appear impressive but collapse when scheduler fairness or interference handling limits usable airtime. Weight what survives congestion, not what dazzles in isolation.

Vendor selection benefits from scenario-based weights because Cisco and Aruba emphasize different strengths that emerge under load. If lecture halls regularly exceed one device per seat, weighted credit for spatial reuse, contention handling, and OFDMA scheduling should increase. Conversely, if the campus spreads across mixed offices with moderate concurrency, backhaul readiness and roaming stability may rank higher. To ground these choices, map a week of real client counts and hourly traffic to an expected airtime budget per cell. Use that baseline to test whether proposed APs can meet targets without exceeding 70 to 80 percent duty during peaks. A margin helps absorb exam weeks or events. Overweighting rare edge features can distract from fundamentals like clean channels and realistic multigig uplinks that prevent wired bottlenecks.

Procurement also needs a migration-aware view, since WiFi 7 coexistence and staged cutovers affect near-term capacity. Many campuses will pilot a few WiFi 8 zones while most buildings remain on earlier standards. In that case, management interoperability and license models influence how quickly you can expand coverage without operational drag. Consider aligning with a roadmap and checklist for readiness to rollout across phases, which keeps RF and switching changes coordinated. The tradeoff is speed versus certainty. Frequently asked questions

Do Cisco and Aruba require new PoE for WiFi 8 access points?

Some higher end models from both vendors need 802.3bt to enable all radios or features. Many models run on 802.3at with reduced capabilities. Check the specific AP data sheet and plan switch power budgets by closet to avoid unexpected downgrades.

Is controller based or cloud management better for large campuses?

Either can scale if operations are well structured. Choose the mode that speeds iteration after observing real traffic. Deep telemetry and automation matter more than where the control plane lives, because faster feedback loops protect airtime and user experience.

How do I estimate the number of WiFi 8 access points needed?

Start with capacity, not coverage. Map peak client counts and target throughput per user, then size cells to keep busy hour duty around 70 to 80 percent. Validate with a pilot in the most demanding rooms, then extrapolate to similar spaces across the campus.

Do I need multigig uplinks on every access switch port?

No. Prioritize multigig for high density rooms and locations with sustained high throughput. Many areas perform well with 2.5 or 5 Gbps on select ports, provided upstream aggregation and QoS are configured to avoid buffer induced retries.

What testing metrics confirm the chosen vendor meets capacity goals?

Track median throughput, 95th percentile latency, retry rates, and channel utilization during busy hours. Accept the design when these metrics meet targets in the worst five percent of rooms, not only in average spaces.
