Best Aspect Ratio on Miyoo Mini: Clarity vs Comfort

By Jeremy Crawford • Published January 3, 2026

The short answer is to use integer scaling for native systems on the Miyoo Mini, then apply a modest fit if you prefer comfort over strict pixel accuracy. This balances aspect ratio fidelity with perceived size and visibility on the small display. Most 8-bit and 16-bit consoles look best at crisp integer multiples with small borders, while handheld systems vary. Short answer and nuance

Start with integer scaling because it maintains the original aspect ratio and pixel grid with no interpolation artifacts. For NES, SNES, Genesis, and similar consoles, integer multiples keep lines straight and text crisp, which is crucial on the Miyoo Mini’s small screen. A common pattern is black bars on one or both axes, which is expected and not a defect. The tradeoff is screen area loss compared with fit-to-screen modes, so sprites appear smaller. If the image feels too tiny during longer sessions, a slight viewport expand can help. The key is to avoid non-integer scaling that introduces uneven pixel widths, since it blurs edges and can cause shimmering during scrolling. Choose integer scaling first, then adjust only if readability truly suffers.

Respect original system geometry before considering a gentle fit because many classic systems use non-square pixels or unusual resolutions. For example, SNES and Genesis games were designed for 4:3 displays, so preserving that shape ensures circles remain circular and characters retain intended proportions. The Miyoo Mini’s panel is not 4:3, so you will typically see side borders with correct 4:3 output. The benefit is faithful artwork and predictable hitboxes. The drawback is perceived emptiness on the sides, which some find distracting. If that bothers you, apply a minor horizontal expand that still keeps circles and squares convincing in a quick test. Avoid aggressive stretch, since it distorts sprites and gradually fatigues the eye during extended play.

Choose fit-to-screen sparingly because it prioritizes comfort over fidelity, which can be appropriate for text-heavy RPGs or strategy games. On the Miyoo Mini, slight expansion can improve readability without ruining geometry if you keep the change restrained. A safe rule of thumb is to nudge the scale until black bars shrink but basic shapes still pass the eye test. This works best on systems where the difference between true aspect and the device screen is modest. The cost is softer lines due to interpolation and the risk of subtle oval shapes. When you notice curves bending or pixel outlines bleeding, revert to integer scaling. Always evaluate several scenes, including menus, dialogue boxes, and scrolling backgrounds.

When it applies vs when it does not

Integer scaling is ideal when a system’s native aspect ratio aligns cleanly with the target display after scaling by whole numbers. For 4:3 console cores, this provides consistent pixel placement and reduces shimmering during scroll. On the Miyoo Mini, expect borders to appear because the panel ratio differs, yet the in-game proportions remain correct. This setting especially shines in action games where crisp edges improve reaction timing and collision clarity. The tradeoff is that HUD text may appear smaller than desired on a compact screen. If small text causes strain, consider a measured viewport increase. Keep the change conservative and verify circles remain round in character portraits or logo screens to maintain trustworthy geometry.

Gentle fit-to-screen can be helpful when legibility is the limiting factor, not accuracy. Genres like tactics or RPGs, where reading dominates moment-to-moment play, benefit from a bit more size on the Miyoo Mini. Tweak scale incrementally and pause to examine curved UI elements, dialogue windows, and inventory grids. If shapes hold up, you gain comfort with minimal harm. The downside is softening introduced by interpolation, most noticeable on fine dithering and diagonal lines. If you notice halos around thin fonts or moire in patterned textures, step back toward a cleaner integer multiple. This approach suits players who prioritize ease of viewing during long sessions more than perfect sprite math.

Frequently asked questions

What aspect ratio should I use for most 4:3 consoles?

Use integer scaling with a 4:3 aspect ratio. This preserves geometry and produces the sharpest image. Expect small borders on the sides due to panel differences. If text is too small, consider a slight increase but keep shapes natural.

Is fit-to-screen a bad idea on Miyoo Mini?

Not always. A gentle fit can improve readability on text-heavy games. Keep expansion conservative and verify circles and squares remain accurate. If you notice distortion or soft edges, revert to integer scaling to protect clarity.

How do I confirm my aspect ratio is correct?

Pause on a screen with circles or square tiles and check shapes for symmetry. Then test a scrolling scene for shimmer or uneven pixel steps. If shapes look natural and motion is stable, save the override for that core.

Should I use smoothing or filters with integer scaling?

Avoid heavy smoothing because it blurs the pixel grid. If borders bother you, try subtle bezels or lightweight shaders that leave pixel edges crisp. Test one effect at a time and watch for performance or latency changes.

Can I set different aspect ratios per system?

Yes. Save per-core overrides in RetroArch after testing a few games for each system. This keeps 4:3 consoles on integer scaling while letting specific handheld cores use different defaults if readability consistently requires it.
