Safe ROM Naming Conventions for Miyoo Mini

By Jeremy Crawford • Published February 27, 2026

Clear ROM naming conventions for the Miyoo Mini reduce scraping errors, prevent duplicate games, and keep lists readable during fast scrolling. This guide explains safe patterns you can trust, why each element matters, and how to balance detail with simplicity. You will learn when to include region tags, how to record revisions or hacks, and what characters to avoid so file systems, scrapers, and emulators behave consistently.

Key takeaways

  • Use a consistent pattern: Title, region tag, and version notes.
  • Avoid special characters that break scrapers or file systems.
  • Prefer standard region tags and short revision markers for clarity.
  • Keep hacks and translations labeled to avoid mistaken matches.
  • Test sample names with your scraper before bulk renaming.

Concepts and definitions

A safe ROM name starts with a human title, followed by a short region code and minimal version notes, because scrapers key off predictable tokens and punctuation. A dependable pattern is Title space open paren region close paren space bracketed note, for example, Super Mario World (USA) [Rev 1]. This balances readability and machine parsing without burying the core title. A tradeoff is verbosity, since long names may wrap in narrow themes, yet overly short names lose important distinctions. Keep region tags to common codes like USA, EUR, or JPN, and reserve bracketed notes for meaningful differences like Rev 1, v1.1, or T-En for fan translations so tools can tell entries apart.

Illegal or risky characters cause issues across file systems and scraping tools, so it is safer to restrict punctuation to spaces, hyphens, parentheses, and brackets. Avoid characters like colon, slash, backslash, asterisk, question mark, and quotes because Windows and FAT32 dislike them and some scrapers misread them. The edge case is titles that include punctuation as part of the brand, like Pokemon or Sonic titles with trademark symbols, which you should omit for portability. Replace colons with a space or hyphen and convert ampersands to and to prevent misparsing. This conservative set preserves meaning while keeping compatibility on SD cards that move between operating systems during setup.

Consistent casing improves scanning speed and grouping across lists, so pick Title Case or proper capitalization that mirrors the original box art and apply it uniformly. Title Case increases readability on small screens and reduces accidental duplicates caused by lowercased variants. The tradeoff is that exact brand stylization like lowercased pokemon may be lost, but consistency benefits outweigh this stylistic preference. Use leading zeros for series numbers only when they sort better, for example, Final Fantasy 01, 02, and 03, though pure alphabetical sort is often sufficient with region tags. When in doubt, prefer the shortest form that still separates regional releases and meaningful updates so your launcher and scraper stay predictable.

Frameworks and decision lenses

Choose a naming pattern by ranking three needs in order: scraping accuracy, library navigation speed, and future portability to other devices. If you scrape box art frequently, keep region tokens close to the title and avoid novelty tags so matches remain stable. If navigation speed matters more, abbreviate notes and remove nonessential tags, since shorter names scroll faster on the Miyoo Mini UI. For portability, use cross-platform safe characters and avoid tool-specific markers that only one scraper understands. A practical baseline is Title space open paren region close paren with optional bracketed notes, and a one-line rule that all games follow. You can refine per system, but the core anchor should not change.

Include bracketed notes only when they disambiguate gameplay or compatibility, because extra tokens increase mismatch risk during art scraping. Good candidates are [Rev 1], [v1.1], [T-En], [Hack], or [MSU-1], since they represent different executables or user experiences. Avoid [GoodSet] style quality tags or redundant releases because those tags clutter lists and do not help the player pick a game. An edge case occurs when translations have multiple versions, like v1.0.1 and v1.2, where you should keep only the one you play. For hacks, append a short descriptor after the tag, for example, [Hack Sonic 2 XL], so similar hacks do not collide. Test ten titles with your scraper to ensure notes do not break matches.

Light automation helps enforce rules at scale without risky mass changes, so start with a dry run that shows proposed new names. A minimal script can normalize whitespace, replace unsafe characters, and append a region if missing, while leaving unknown tokens untouched. The main risk is overfitting patterns that accidentally rename non ROM files like images, so filter by known extensions such as .nes, .sfc, .gba, .gb, or .zip. Keep backups or work on a copy until results are stable. Looking ahead

Standardized names only help if they stay consistent, so plan a light maintenance pass whenever adding new ROMs or updating your library. Keep a short checklist that verifies region tags, trims risky characters, and consolidates duplicate variants so the list remains clean. A workable rhythm is reviewing new additions in a staging folder, running your dry run script, and moving results into the ROMs directory. This reduces surprises during scraping and keeps thumbnails aligned. If your SD structure needs attention, pair naming work with a tidy folder layout that groups systems and avoids deep nesting by consulting a guide that explains format and folder reliability tips for the Miyoo Mini SD card.

Scraping mismatches still occur with edge cases like multi disc sets, compilation carts, or hacks that share titles with originals, so use cautious notes and validate art on a small pilot set before scaling. For multi part releases, a suffix such as Disc 1 or Side B after the region tag keeps order without confusing scrapers. Compilations often benefit from the compilation tag in brackets, but avoid listing every included game in the filename since that bloats menus. When a mismatch persists, prefer manual art assignment over exotic tags that may break other tools later. Stability across devices beats perfect specificity that only one database understands.

As firmware and scraping databases improve, revisit these conventions once or twice a year rather than constantly tuning names. The goal is predictable matches, quick list scanning, and minimal touch time when migrating to a new SD card or handheld. Future compatibility is easier when your pattern uses safe characters and short, widely recognized tokens for region and revisions. Keep a text file in the ROMs root describing your rules for anyone who contributes files to the shared library. This small step prevents drift and reduces the chance that art breaks after an update or a move to a larger card or a different frontend.
