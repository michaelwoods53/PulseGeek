Fix Audio Crackling or Stutter in DuckStation

By Brian Rogers • Published February 23, 2026

Audio stutter usually begins with timing problems, not volume or speakers. In DuckStation, crackling is often caused by buffer underruns, mismatched sync, or frame pacing hiccups that starve the audio thread. This guide targets realistic causes and shows how to fix crackling with latency buffers, backend selection, and synchronization settings. We assume a standard desktop or handheld PC, default DuckStation build, and no exotic audio middleware. If you use Bluetooth or USB headsets, we will account for added latency and potential driver quirks. You will pick a path based on whether your GPU is stable at full speed, then confirm results with simple tests and targeted tweaks that remove clicks without adding noticeable delay.

Key takeaways

  • Crackling usually means buffer underruns from timing or sync mismatch.
  • Increase audio latency gradually until stutter stops without lip lag.
  • Match video sync to stable frame pacing to protect audio continuity.
  • Choose a reliable backend and avoid exclusive modes on shared devices.
  • Verify with looped scenes and input taps to detect hidden drift.

Plan the work

Define the success condition before changing anything, because audio settings are best tuned to a target. For most players, the goal is continuous playback with clean effects and speech that aligns with animation within a small tolerance. A practical threshold is no audible crackles in 60 seconds of busy scenes and no more than a barely noticeable delay when pressing buttons during menu sounds. Establish your platform constraints too. For example, Bluetooth adds latency and can mask stutter, while USB DACs may cause exclusive-mode conflicts. Planning upfront avoids random toggles that trade one artifact for another. By stating a clear pass or fail, you can stop when the audio is stable rather than chasing perfection that hurts responsiveness.

Choose a tuning path based on rendering stability, because audio relies on predictable timing from the video thread. If your GPU can hold full speed without dips, aim to lock video sync and keep latency modest for responsive controls. If your frame rate fluctuates, prioritize larger buffers to shield the audio thread from underruns. This forked approach sets expectations about tradeoffs. Low latency feels great when the system is steady, but it can expose clicks when frames stutter. Higher buffers glide through turbulence at the cost of a small input-to-sound delay. By declaring which path you are on, you prevent circular tweaks and focus only on settings that match your system behavior.

Inventory your environment to reduce unknowns that hide real causes of crackle. Note the audio output device, whether it is HDMI, analog, Bluetooth, or USB, and identify any system-wide enhancements like spatial audio or third-party mixers. These layers may resample or inject effects that alter timing. Also record whether VSync, frame limiters, or screen capture overlays are active. Overlays and capture tools can interrupt presentation timing, which propagates into the audio queue. Finally, jot down your DuckStation version and baseline settings so you can revert if needed. Having a snapshot makes experimentation safe, helps reproduce outcomes, and prevents accidental compounding of changes when troubleshooting later.

Prepare environment

Stabilize the operating system audio path first, since emulator changes cannot fix driver-level glitches. Set your OS sample rate to a common value such as 48 kHz to avoid unnecessary resampling, then restart apps that touch sound. Disable spatial effects or enhancements temporarily, especially on Bluetooth or HDMI, because layered processing can lengthen buffers and confuse sync. If you use a USB DAC or gaming headset, confirm its firmware and driver are current. Finally, close background apps that hook audio, including voice chat or browser tabs with running media. These steps reduce jitter sources that can cause subtle underruns, which you might mistakenly attribute to DuckStation settings.

Choose the output device that matches your use case, because device-specific quirks change latency and stability. Wired headphones or speakers through a motherboard output tend to be the most predictable for low-latency tuning. HDMI often adds a few frames of pipeline delay which can hide small clicks but complicate lip sync. Bluetooth typically increases latency and may drift with radio interference. If your testing requires strict responsiveness, avoid wireless during initial configuration. You can introduce Bluetooth later and adjust buffers upward until crackles disappear. By sequencing devices from most to least stable, you isolate problems quickly and avoid chasing issues that are really transport related.

Prepare DuckStation for controlled tests by resetting only audio and timing settings to known defaults. Avoid global resets that would discard graphics tuning unless you have a full backup. Set frame limiter enabled, VSync as appropriate for your renderer, and leave exotic features off until the base path is clean. Create a short save state in a busy scene with music and sound effects, ideally a loop you can replay. This creates a consistent stress test across changes. Plan to test for a full minute per change, because short clips can miss intermittent underruns. The discipline of one change at a time prevents compounding effects and makes the first good configuration stick.

Execute steps

Work through fixes in a stable sequence, because the order matters for isolating crackling causes. Begin with synchronization choices that affect both video and audio timing, then expand to audio backend and buffer size. Only after timing is steady should you adjust optional features like time stretching that can mask problems by altering pitch processing. This laddered approach lets you detect when a change upstream resolves the issue, which avoids unnecessary compromises further down. If the system maintains full speed, aim for moderate latency. If it dips, pick conservative buffers that do not crackle under stress. You will validate each step with a consistent loop so results are comparable.

As you tune, leverage renderer decisions explained in related guidance, since graphics pacing drives audio continuity. Switching to Vulkan can help some systems reduce frame time spikes, which protects the audio queue. If you are still setting up the emulator, review a structured setup path for BIOS and game paths to ensure no background scanning interferes during tests. When you later chase visual fidelity such as resolution scaling or PGXP, move stepwise and retest audio, because heavier graphics can alter timing. Keeping visuals stable during audio tuning is the quickest path to a lasting fix that survives further configuration.

Validate results

Prove that your settings survive real gameplay, not just menus, because many underruns appear only during effects heavy moments. Use a looped action scene and listen for at least a minute while watching input-driven sounds such as cursor moves or jump effects. The absence of clicks across multiple overlapping sounds indicates a healthy buffer margin. Then pause and resume quickly. Some systems crackle at resume due to device wake-ups or sample rate renegotiation. If you hear a single click at resume but not during playback, note it and continue. You can often ignore that harmless blip if the rest of the session stays clean.

Cross-check responsiveness using a simple rhythm test, since audio smoothness should not come at the cost of mushy controls. Tap a menu repeatedly and judge whether the sound lands close to your input. If the delay feels long, lower the buffer a small step and retest. Stop precisely where stability and responsiveness meet. If both cannot be satisfied, prefer stability for story-driven games and responsiveness for precision play. Create two profiles if your workflow supports it, so you can load the appropriate balance without retuning each time. This preserves consistency and prevents drift from creeping back into your default configuration.

Verify that changes do not conflict with visual features you plan to enable, because later graphics tweaks can reintroduce stutter. After locking a stable audio configuration, toggle your preferred renderer and resolution scaling one at a time. Re-run the looped test for each change. If a visual enhancement causes occasional ticks, either raise the audio buffer slightly or back off the visual setting until frame pacing steadies. For systems on the edge, consider adopting a renderer choice that reduces spikes. Switching to a different API can improve consistency even when average frame rate appears unchanged.

Troubleshoot and optimize

Tackle recurring crackles by identifying the trigger rather than raising buffers blindly. If clicks happen during scene transitions or when switching focus, look for overlays or capture tools that hook the presentation path. Disable them during play or configure a lower impact capture mode. If the issue appears only on Bluetooth, test with wired output to confirm radio interference and codec latency as the cause. In that case, accept a slightly larger buffer or switch to a low latency codec supported by your device. Matching the remedy to the trigger prevents overcompensation that would make every sound feel sluggish.

When drivers fight for the device, switch backend or release exclusive access to ease contention. Some systems behave more reliably with a different audio API, and shared mode avoids conflicts with browsers or chat tools. If another app must stay open, set that app to a fixed sample rate that matches your OS rate to reduce resampling churn. Consider dedicating one output device to DuckStation during long sessions and routing other apps elsewhere. The separation keeps timing intact under mixed workloads. Always document what combination you are using so you can revert later without guesswork.

If you continue to hear clicks even with conservative buffers, suspect frame pacing spikes. Revisit renderer choices and confirm they align with your hardware. Many users benefit from a lower overhead API that reduces micro stutter on certain GPUs. For deeper graphics tuning, follow detailed guidance on recommended settings and timing options to remove intermittent judder that often manifests as audio artifacts. Once graphics pacing improves, you can usually dial buffers down a notch while keeping sound smooth. This last step unlocks responsiveness without sacrificing the crackle-free baseline you established earlier.

Key terms

  • Audio Buffer — Audio samples queued ahead to prevent crackling or dropouts.
  • Audio Latency — Delay between audio events and playback.
  • Audio routing — Selecting which devices handle output and input audio.
  • Frame Rate Cap — A set limit on frames per second to control performance and power.

Frequently asked questions

Why does audio crackle only when I alt tab?

Switching focus can interrupt the presentation pipeline or reinitialize the audio device. That brief stall can cause an underrun. Reduce overlays, avoid exclusive modes, and consider a slightly larger buffer to ride out focus changes.

Should I always use the lowest possible audio latency?

No. Extremely low buffers expose underruns during frame time spikes. Use the smallest buffer that remains crackle free in a one minute looped scene. If gameplay feels delayed, step down one notch and retest to balance feel and stability.

Will a different renderer fix audio stutter by itself?

Sometimes. A renderer that produces steadier frame times reduces the chance of starving the audio thread. If audio clicks persist after switching, increase the buffer a small amount. Validate again using a repeatable scene with mixed sounds.

Do Bluetooth headsets always cause crackling?

No, but they add latency and can drift with interference. If crackles occur, test with wired output to confirm the cause. If wired is clean, keep Bluetooth and raise the buffer until artifacts stop, accepting the added delay as a tradeoff.

Why does enabling VSync change audio behavior?

VSync changes frame pacing, which can smooth or disrupt timing depending on your GPU and display. If enabling VSync removes crackles, keep it. If it adds stutter, disable it and increase the audio buffer to protect the queue instead.
