Why Is Audio Crackling in RetroArch on Miyoo?

By Jared Butler • Published February 12, 2026

Audio crackling on Miyoo with RetroArch usually points to a timing mismatch or an overloaded buffer rather than a broken device. Pops appear when audio cannot be filled fast enough or when video timing fights the audio clock. The fix starts by aligning sync choices and right sizing buffers so audio frames arrive steadily. This guide answers why crackling happens, when certain features make it worse, and how to correct it with practical RetroArch settings. We also validate improvements with quick tests so changes are deliberate. Along the way, we connect latency features like runahead and frame pacing to crackle risk, and we show where to keep ambition in check on Miyoo’s limited headroom.

Key takeaways

  • Crackling usually stems from unstable timing or too small audio buffers.
  • Enable audio rate control to let sound track video timing safely.
  • Increase audio latency modestly when cores or shaders raise workload.
  • Validate changes by monitoring dropped frames and listening for pops.

Short answer and nuance

The short answer is that audio crackling happens when RetroArch’s audio thread cannot receive data at a steady cadence, usually because video sync or emulation load disrupts timing. On Miyoo hardware, modest CPU and GPU budgets make timing slippage more frequent during heavy scenes, shader use, or aggressive latency settings. A quick fix is enabling audio rate control, which slightly adjusts playback rate to match video timing, then setting a conservative audio latency value so the buffer can absorb small spikes. This combination reduces underruns without making sound feel loose. The tradeoff is marginal pitch or tempo drift that is typically inaudible. If pops persist, the problem may be deeper, like a core running below full speed or a sync mode that continually starves audio.

Audio rate control deserves a clear mental model because it often resolves pops without brute force buffers. RetroArch can gently vary the audio clock within a narrow skew range so it tracks the video clock and avoids repeated buffer underflow. Think of it as a fine trim that keeps streams aligned rather than a large correction. On Miyoo, this works well when your frame times are mostly stable and only occasionally spiky. However, if the emulator never reaches full speed, rate control cannot conjure missing frames, and you will still hear artifacts. In that case, step one is restoring consistent frame delivery by lowering core load, disabling heavy shaders, or recalibrating latency options that push the device too far.

A second nuance is that video sync choices and queues indirectly shape audio stability. For instance, strict vsync combined with very small frame delay can tighten input feel but leave no margin for slow frames, which increases audio underruns. Conversely, looser queues or triple buffering might smooth delivery but introduce latency that undermines control. On Miyoo, the sweet spot is modest buffering and conservative frame delay so frames complete on time while audio rate control bridges minor drift. If you also use features like runahead, set it conservatively and only for cores that tolerate it well. The guiding principle is coordination across settings so the audio thread never waits longer than the buffer can cover.

When it applies vs when it does not

Audio rate control and modest buffers work best when games already run at full speed and only short spikes cause instability. In that case, allowing a tiny playback skew and giving audio a bit more queue time stops pops without noticeable lag. Typical scenarios include 2D cores with occasional effects or brief shader transitions. The rule of thumb is that if performance graphs show mostly steady frame times with rare blips, rate control carries the day. By contrast, if you apply heavy runahead, rewind, or multi pass shaders, the system may spend long stretches over budget. In that world, buffer tweaks feel like band aids, and the correct action is to reduce load before caring about audio trims.

These fixes do not apply when the emulator consistently misses frame deadlines or when vsync configuration conflicts with the display’s refresh. On handheld screens, a mismatch between core output and panel timing can cause constant resampling pressure and repeating underruns. If your performance overlay shows frequent dropped frames or percentage speed below 100, prioritize restoring full speed by disabling demanding effects, lowering runahead count, or turning off rewind. Only after frame delivery is stable should you fine tune audio. If you tune buffers first, you risk masking the root cause while keeping stutter elsewhere, which feels worse than a small pop during a single spike.

Finally, know when to use adjacent features that affect timing. Hard GPU sync and extreme frame delay can reduce perceived input lag, yet on Miyoo they cut slack needed to fill audio buffers. If your priority is crackle free playback, choose stability over the last few milliseconds by softening frame delay and keeping GPU sync conservative. For players chasing minimal lag in rhythm or precision titles, accept that some audio risk returns and mitigate with slightly larger audio latency. Frequently asked questions

Should I raise audio latency or enable rate control first?

Enable audio rate control first, then raise audio latency modestly if pops remain. This keeps sync aligned while adding just enough buffer to ride out small spikes.

Why does crackling happen more with shaders or rewind on?

Shaders and rewind add CPU and memory load, which increases frame time variance. When frames arrive late, the audio buffer underruns, producing clicks or pops.

Does triple buffering fix audio crackle on Miyoo?

It can smooth video but often adds latency without solving audio underruns. If performance is under budget, stabilizing frame delivery works better than extra buffering.

Will runahead always cause audio pops?

No, conservative runahead can be stable when the system has headroom. High counts on limited hardware raise the risk of underruns that manifest as crackles.

Which config lines matter most for stopping pops?

Turn on audio rate control, cap maximum timing skew, keep video vsync enabled, and set a moderate audio latency. These protect audio from brief timing stalls.
