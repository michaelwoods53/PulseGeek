Best RetroArch Shaders for Miyoo Mini (Lightweight)

By Jared Butler • Published January 4, 2026

Choosing RetroArch shaders for a Miyoo Mini is a balancing act between aesthetics and performance. This guide selects lightweight shaders that preserve crisp pixels, add subtle CRT or LCD texture, and keep navigation responsive. Items were chosen through practical tuning principles like integer scaling first, gentle passes over complex stacks, and presets known for low overhead on modest hardware. You will see concrete examples, quick tuning rules, and the tradeoffs that come with each approach so your shaders complement long play sessions rather than slow them down.

Key takeaways

  • Favor single-pass shaders and integer scaling to preserve smooth performance.
  • Start from sharp-bilinear or zfast variants, then layer minimal effects.
  • Avoid heavy multi-pass CRT stacks that add latency and frame drops.
  • Use per-game presets for odd resolutions or scaling edge cases.
  • Pair shaders with overlays sparingly to keep the screen readable.

1) Sharp Bilinear Simple

Use sharp bilinear as the baseline when you want RetroArch shaders to add clarity with almost no cost. This pass respects integer scaling, keeps edges clean, and avoids the plastic look that pure bilinear filtering can introduce. For example, apply integer scale with borders and then stack a single sharp-bilinear pass to preserve crisp pixels on the Miyoo display while softening the harshness of nearest neighbor. The tradeoff is aesthetic minimalism. You will not get scanlines or mask texture, and subpixel wobble from non-integer sizes still shows. The why is simple. A single-pass sharpener does not reorder pixels aggressively, so it keeps latency predictable and frame time low. Start here before testing heavier CRT looks to make sure the baseline feels responsive in menus and gameplay.

Section highlights

  • Begin with sharp bilinear to keep frames steady and edges crisp.
  • Layer effects later only if responsiveness remains consistently solid.

2) zfast CRT

Pick zfast_crt when you want a gentle CRT flavor that stays light. The pass aims for faint scanlines and mild bloom without heavy mask simulation, which helps the Miyoo keep consistent timing. As a concrete example, pair integer scale with zfast_crt and reduce scanline strength to around a subtle level so text remains readable in RPGs. The tradeoff is authenticity versus speed. zfast does not simulate phosphor triads or advanced beam shapes, so purists may find it less convincing. The advantage is predictable performance and minimal configuration. The mechanism is straightforward. With fewer calculations per pixel and no multipass stack, the shader avoids the overhead of complex kernels. Use zfast when you want a tasteful hint of CRT while keeping response sharp for action titles.

Section highlights

  • zfast adds faint scanlines and glow without heavy multipass costs.
  • Trim scanline strength to maintain UI readability on small screens.

3) zfast LCD

Choose zfast_lcd when you prefer a subtle LCD matrix look that tightens pixel edges. This shader adds a restrained grid that can stabilize diagonals and reduce shimmer, which helps platformers and puzzle games on the Miyoo. For example, enable integer scaling, then apply zfast_lcd and nudge grid intensity down until fine UI elements remain crisp. The tradeoff is that an LCD grid can exaggerate moire on certain color combinations or at fractional scales. The why is practical. A simple per-pixel modulation avoids expensive neighborhood sampling, so the pass stays quick while providing a consistent texture. Use it if you enjoy a modern handheld aesthetic over tube emulation and you want a low-effort setup that rarely needs per-game changes beyond small intensity adjustments.

Section highlights

  • zfast_lcd adds a light grid that stabilizes edges at integer scale.
  • Lower intensity to prevent moire patterns on busy backgrounds.

4) CRT Guest Advance Fastest

Use the fastest preset from the crt-guest family when you want a credible CRT taste with tight constraints. On the Miyoo Mini, the slim variant avoids multi-pass masks and focuses on gentle beam shaping and light scanlines. As an example, load the fastest preset, then reduce mask or bloom settings that can muddy small text. The tradeoff is nuance. Compared to full guest advanced stacks, you lose detailed mask patterns and dynamic beam width. The benefit is a stable frame time that suits action games where rapid inputs matter. The why comes down to arithmetic complexity. Limiting calculations per pixel and minimizing passes reduces overhead while preserving a familiar tube feel that many players prefer for 8 and 16 bit consoles.

Section highlights

  • Use the fastest guest preset for tasteful CRT without heavy masks.
  • Dial back bloom to keep small fonts legible on Miyoo screens.

5) CRT Pi

Try crt-pi for a friendly CRT preset designed with low power hardware in mind. It creates soft scanlines and a balanced gamma curve without layering complex subpixel masks, which suits the Miyoo Mini’s modest GPU. For example, set integer scale, apply crt-pi, and trim scanline darkness for RPG battle menus where heavy lines hide thin UI separators. The tradeoff is realism versus readability. crt-pi favors clarity and speed, so it will not mimic phosphor triads or shadow masks closely. The reason it works well is its conservative approach. Limiting parameters reduces both configuration time and per-frame work. It is a solid everyday choice when you want a gentle CRT vibe that rarely needs per-game tweaks and stays steady during long sessions.

Section highlights

  • crt-pi balances gentle scanlines with fast performance on handhelds.
  • Lighten scanlines for menus and text-heavy games to improve clarity.

6) Easymode Lite

Pick a lite Easymode variant when you want the classic scanline look with minimal setup. Easymode focuses on readable contrast and controlled bloom rather than complex aperture masks, so it adapts well to the Miyoo Mini. For example, start with a lite preset, reduce halation if highlights look smeared, and keep scanlines moderate so 1 pixel UI outlines remain visible. The tradeoff is texture subtlety. If you crave precise CRT mask behavior, Easymode lite will feel too smooth. The strength of this path is consistency. With fewer dials to turn and a restrained effect, you can keep a single preset across several systems and only adjust gamma per core. It is a dependable middle ground between sharp-bilinear and heavier CRT stacks.

Section highlights

  • Easymode lite favors clarity with gentle scanlines and limited bloom.
  • Lower halation to prevent highlight smear on bright UI elements.

7) Scanlines Only

Use a scanlines only pass when absolute speed and readability matter more than authenticity. This approach overlays clean horizontal lines while leaving pixel structure intact, which keeps the Miyoo Mini responsive even in busy scenes. For instance, pair integer scaling with a simple scanline shader and keep line opacity below a modest threshold so thin fonts are not crushed. The tradeoff is that the image can feel flat without beam or mask simulation. The reason it works is arithmetic simplicity. A single modulation step per row adds almost no cost. This is a great fallback when more stylized options introduce judder in scrolling shooters or platformers. If you later add an overlay bezel, keep opacity gentle to avoid stacking darkness with the scanline effect.

Section highlights

  • Single-pass scanlines preserve speed and keep edges straightforward.
  • Keep opacity modest to protect thin UI lines from crushing.

8) Scale2x Family

Consider scale2x or similar families when sprite edges look too stair-stepped but you want to keep latency tight. These passes round diagonals and smooth pixel art without heavy pattern detection, which suits the Miyoo Mini. As an example, apply scale2x at a modest strength, then layer sharp-bilinear to recover definition in text. The tradeoff is that aggressive smoothing can blur fine HUD elements and soften tiles that rely on crisp squares. The mechanism is deterministic edge expansion rather than complex neural or multi-pass upscalers, so cost stays low. Use this path for handheld-centric titles or games with bold sprites where a touch of softness improves motion clarity without hiding animation frames. Avoid stacking with thick scanlines, which can make the image muddy.

Section highlights

  • scale2x gently smooths diagonals while keeping computational cost low.
  • Combine with sharp-bilinear to restore definition in text-heavy scenes.

9) FXAA Light

Use a light FXAA pass to tame shimmer when scrolling backgrounds create aliasing that distracts. On the Miyoo Mini, a restrained FXAA setting can smooth high-contrast edges while preserving integer-scaled structure. For example, enable integer scaling, then apply a single FXAA pass and keep the threshold conservative so text remains crisp in menu-driven games. The tradeoff is that FXAA can blur thin lines if pushed too far, especially on fonts designed around 1 pixel strokes. The why is algorithmic. FXAA evaluates contrast at edges and blends selectively, which is cheaper than multi-sample methods. Keep it as a finishing touch and avoid pairing it with heavy scanlines, since combined softening may dull sprites more than intended. It works best on fast action where shimmer is noticeable.

Section highlights

  • FXAA reduces distracting shimmer when backgrounds scroll quickly.
  • Use conservative thresholds to avoid blurring one pixel text.

10) LCD Grid Mini

Try a mini LCD grid preset when you want a fine texture that complements small screens. This approach adds a tight, low-contrast matrix that can stabilize diagonal motion while staying light enough for the Miyoo. For example, select a preset with a small cell size and dial opacity down so busy scenes do not show moire. The tradeoff is sensitivity to scaling ratios. At fractional sizes, grids can clash with pixel patterns and introduce shimmer. The mechanism relies on a simple periodic modulation that is inexpensive compared to simulated masks or beam dynamics. Use it when you prefer a modern LCD feel and want a consistent look across different 8 and 16 bit cores. Keep brightness balanced if you also use a bezel overlay to avoid stacking darkness.

Section highlights

  • A fine LCD grid adds texture with minimal overhead on handhelds.
  • Reduce opacity to limit moire on fractional or awkward scales.

11) Baseline No Shader + Overlay

Keep a no-shader baseline with integer scaling and a tasteful overlay for times when every frame counts. This setup defines your control state, ensures sharp pixels, and lets you add design through a bezel without compute cost, which suits the Miyoo Mini for long sessions. For example, set integer scale and aspect per core, then apply a thin bezel overlay with low opacity. The tradeoff is minimal stylization. You gain stability and legibility but lose CRT or LCD texture. The reason to keep this baseline is diagnostic. It shows whether slowdowns come from shaders or other settings, and it pairs well with hotkeys for quick comparisons. Frequently asked questions

Which shader should I try first on Miyoo Mini?

Start with sharp-bilinear as a baseline, then test zfast_crt or crt-pi. Keep scanlines modest and verify menus and scrolling scenes feel smooth before trying heavier looks.

Do shaders add input lag on low power devices?

Complex multi-pass stacks can add latency or frame drops. Single-pass options like sharp-bilinear, zfast variants, or crt-pi typically keep timing steady when paired with integer scaling.

Are GLSL or slang shaders better for Miyoo Mini?

Use the format supported by your RetroArch build. Many presets exist in both forms with similar looks. Choose the lightweight variant and confirm performance per game.

When should I disable a shader entirely?

If you notice stutter during busy scenes or unreadable text, switch to a no-shader baseline with integer scale. Reintroduce a single-pass shader and retest until performance is stable.

Can overlays and shaders be used together safely?

Yes, but keep overlay opacity low and avoid thick scanlines with dark bezels. Combining both can reduce brightness and readability on small screens.
