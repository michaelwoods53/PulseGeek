Bilinear Filtering vs Sharp Scaling on Miyoo

By Austin Price • Published February 2, 2026

Bilinear filtering and sharp scaling shape how retro games feel on Miyoo. The decision hinges on pixel clarity, stability, and input latency, not just aesthetics. We will compare smoothing versus integer-based scaling through clarity, timing, and performance, then map these to common systems and screen ratios. The main lens focuses on consistent motion and readable sprites, because that drives both comfort and accuracy during action games. Where relevant, we will point to safe RetroArch toggles, like video smoothing and integer scale, that you can enable without overclocking. If you also tune per-core overrides and sync, as explained in our guide to per-core overrides and video threads, you can keep settings stable while switching systems without surprises.

Key takeaways

  • Sharp integer scaling delivers crisp pixels and predictable input timing.
  • Bilinear filtering softens subpixel shimmer but reduces small text clarity.
  • Prefer sharp scaling for action titles where precision and edges matter.
  • Use bilinear when fractional scaling causes shimmer during slow camera pans.
  • Set per core overrides to keep scaling choices consistent across systems.

Evaluation criteria and how to weigh them

Start by weighting clarity, latency, and performance based on what you play. Clarity means how legible sprites, HUD icons, and text remain when scaled from native resolutions to the Miyoo screen. As a rule of thumb, if you value pixel art fidelity and accurate dither patterns, assign clarity the highest weight and bias toward integer scaling. Latency covers input and visual response, where smoothing rarely helps timing but integer scaling avoids extra processing. Performance includes power draw and frame pacing stability. On Miyoo, both modes are lightweight, yet smoothing can hide micro stutter while blurring fine edges. The tradeoff is softness on small fonts, which matters for RPG menus and handheld-origin titles with compact UI.

Next, define how fractional scaling affects your library. Systems that map cleanly to the display through whole-number scale factors benefit most from sharp integer scaling because pixels align predictably. For consoles with awkward aspect ratios or resolutions that produce fractional scales, bilinear filtering can reduce shimmering during scrolling backgrounds. For example, a Genesis title with heavy horizontal parallax may exhibit subpixel crawl when scaled fractionally, and smoothing can mask that without changing timing. The limitation is that masking does not fix aliasing on diagonal lines, so overly soft edges may hinder aiming in shooters. Your weighting should reflect how often these fractional edge cases occur across your favorite systems.

Finally, consider session length and comfort. Long handheld sessions magnify minor readability issues and recurrent shimmer. If you often play text-heavy RPGs or strategy games, prioritize crispness so font edges remain stable across hours of use. For chill platformers or exploration where precise edges are less critical, a touch of smoothing can feel easier on the eyes during slow pans. Also account for your tolerance to post-processing. Some players perceive even mild blur as reduced detail, leading to fatigue of a different kind. Because Miyoo has modest hardware, pick settings that you seldom need to toggle. Use per-core overrides so decisions persist per system and reduce cognitive load between games.

Deep dives per attribute with tradeoffs and concrete examples

Clarity starts with native resolution alignment. Integer scaling maps source pixels to whole-number multiples, so Game Boy and many 8-bit systems render with exact edges. This makes dithering patterns and sprite outlines read correctly, which helps during high-contrast scenes. The drawback appears when the screen does not fit an integer multiple, producing border space or requiring fractional fill that can shimmer during scrolling. Bilinear filtering softens that shimmer by averaging adjacent pixels, which can feel smoother on backgrounds with subpixel motion. The cost is softened text and less distinct single-pixel highlights. If precise edges matter for your playstyle or you rely on tiny HUD elements, sharp scaling usually wins even if you accept small borders.

Timing and latency favor simplicity. Both modes are inexpensive on Miyoo, but sharp integer scaling removes a post-process interpolation pass that can vary subtly by driver. While the difference is often below perception, predictable paths help when you are also using input tweaks like runahead. If you are experimenting with runahead to reduce input delay safely, keep your visual pipeline deterministic so you can attribute changes to one variable at a time. Should you need a quick fix for fractional shimmer without changing aspect, try bilinear until you stabilize your per-core settings. This isolates variables and helps diagnose whether stutter stems from sync, threading, or the scaling choice.

Configuration in RetroArch is straightforward. Turn on Integer Scale for sharp mapping and leave Video Smoothing disabled. For bilinear, disable integer scaling and enable smoothing. Save per-core overrides so each system opens with the right preference without manual toggling. If a platform renders at awkward resolutions, consider adjusting aspect to the content’s native ratio or using a small bezel to preserve integer scaling while filling space gracefully. When you evaluate performance, check for micro stutters during slow pans rather than only fast action. That is where smoothing’s masking benefit appears most clearly and where crisp pixels can reveal subpixel crawl that distracts some players.

Fit by scenario with short, explicit recommendations

Action platformers and shooters benefit most from sharp integer scaling. The clear edges improve hit confirmation and make thin bullets or hazards easier to track. If you play systems that map cleanly to the screen with whole-number scaling, this choice stays crisp without shimmer. When fractional fill is unavoidable, accept small borders or a bezel rather than blur edges. For RPGs with heavy text and menus, sharp scaling also helps because font strokes remain distinct. An exception is when scrolling backgrounds exhibit visible crawl that draws your attention. In that case, try bilinear temporarily to see if the motion becomes more comfortable without affecting input response, then decide per core via overrides.

Parallax-rich 16-bit titles and some handheld-to-console ports often show subpixel motion that can shimmer at fractional scales. If that shimmer distracts you more than softened edges, bilinear filtering is a practical compromise. Keep an eye on HUD and dialogue text to ensure legibility remains acceptable at your preferred viewing distance. If you find text too soft, consider enabling integer scaling and adjusting aspect or borders to re-center content. Frequently asked questions

Does bilinear filtering increase input lag on Miyoo?

For most users it does not add meaningful delay. Both bilinear and integer scaling are lightweight, and input timing depends more on core speed and sync settings than on the smoothing pass.

Will integer scaling always eliminate shimmer?

No. Integer scaling prevents resampling artifacts, but if the content or aspect requires fractional fill or scrolling introduces subpixel motion, you may still notice crawl. Borders or a bezel can help preserve clarity.

Which option uses less power?

The difference is typically small. Both modes run efficiently on Miyoo. Power draw changes more with frame rate stability and audio sync choices than with the selected scaling method.

Should I set scaling globally or per core?

Use a sensible global default, then save per-core overrides for systems that need exceptions. This keeps switching friction low while preserving the right look where fractional scaling would be distracting.
