Factory Reset vs Soft Reset on Miyoo Mini

By Jeremy Crawford • Published March 8, 2026

Choosing between a factory reset and a soft reset on Miyoo Mini starts with risk tolerance and the problem you face. A soft reset clears transient states without wiping files, while a factory reset returns the device to a baseline and may remove customizations. We will compare data impact, time cost, and how Onion OS or RetroArch settings behave under each choice. The goal is simple clarity that reduces guesswork and avoids lost saves. We will also provide a side by side table and then walk attribute by attribute, so you can act with confidence and avoid repeating resets unnecessarily.

Key takeaways

  • Use a soft reset first for freezes, audio glitches, or menu hangs.
  • Reserve a factory reset for persistent faults that survive reboots.
  • Back up saves and BIOS files before any deeper reset action.
  • Reapply Onion OS tweaks after factory reset to restore performance.
  • Confirm SD card health to avoid repeating the same reset.

Evaluation criteria and how to weigh them

Start with data risk because reset choices differ most on what they erase. A soft reset keeps SD contents intact and only reinitializes running processes, which suits freezes after a long play session. A factory reset may roll back settings or require reconfiguring Onion OS features, which risks losing custom RetroArch overlays if not backed up. A reliable rule of thumb is back up saves and BIOS files before anything deeper than a restart. The tradeoff is time. A quick soft reset takes under a minute, while a factory reset can take fifteen to thirty minutes including checks. Choose the safer option first when the issue is transient and escalate only if the fault returns.

Consider root cause clarity next because soft resets help confirm whether the fault is temporary. If screen tearing disappears after a soft reset and stays gone, the cause was likely a resource conflict, not corrupted assets. If stutter returns after two reboots, perform deeper validation such as SD integrity tests before a factory reset. This sequence avoids needless reinstallation work and preserves Onion OS tweaks. The limitation is that intermittent faults can mask underlying storage problems. To mitigate, check for repeatable triggers, like a specific emulator core or playlist action, and log the moment you see the glitch to guide escalation.

Evaluate recovery friction by counting steps required after each path. A soft reset usually needs no post work, aside from reopening a core in RetroArch. A factory reset may require reimporting configurations, setting per core options, and restoring hotkeys. That friction matters when you maintain multiple profiles or rotate SD cards. A practical yardstick is the restore budget. If you cannot allocate time to reapply settings today, pause and attempt a lighter approach. An edge case is a misapplied theme or shell tweak that loops on boot. In that case, a factory reset may be the only practical path to a clean state because the UI cannot load to accept a soft reset.

Deep dives per attribute with tradeoffs and concrete examples

Data impact determines how much you must rebuild, so treat it carefully. A soft reset restarts processes and clears memory pressure without touching files, which is ideal when RetroArch menus stall after swapping cores. A factory reset may revert settings folders and disable custom hotkeys, which costs time and invites errors if you forget BIOS paths. The upside is a clean slate when a theme or launcher change causes crashes at boot. An edge case appears when saves live in an unexpected directory. Before resetting, verify save and state paths in RetroArch to avoid confusion later. This is why backing up saves and BIOS files to a PC or second card is the safest baseline for either option.

Time to complete reflects both the reset and the restore overhead. A soft reset is quick because it relies on restarting services, not rewriting configuration. This makes it the first step during travel or when battery is low. A factory reset absorbs more time because you need to reapply Onion OS options like button remaps and per platform settings. When planning, budget extra minutes to test a few cores for audio and input correctness. The risk is missing a setting that only shows up during a specific workflow, like fast forward stutter. To counter that, keep a simple validation checklist you can run in five minutes.

Post steps vary by depth and should be explicit before you start. After a soft reset, reopen the game, check audio levels, and confirm save write on exit. After a factory reset, restore configuration files, confirm BIOS checksums if applicable, and test network time if your setup uses it. Reapply game list sorting and overlays to maintain a consistent experience across consoles. A limitation is human drift. Without notes, you might misremember a scaling option and interpret the new look as a bug. To avoid that, capture screenshots of key menus before you reset so you can compare views during restore and catch mismatches quickly.

Fit by scenario with short, explicit recommendations

For random freezes during long sessions, favor a soft reset first because it clears temporary resource contention. Example: audio crackles after pausing a PlayStation game then browsing menus. A quick restart of the device or UI processes typically restores smooth output. If the issue resolves and does not repeat after another hour, you are done. If it returns the same day, widen your check to emulator core settings and consider targeted maintenance like clearing shaders. When you see repeated flicker or tearing symptoms, apply a focused remedy that addresses the display behavior rather than immediately switching to a factory reset path.

For repeated crashes on boot or when opening the launcher, escalate to a factory reset because configuration corruption is likely. This is common after theme experiments or partial updates that leave mismatched files. Before starting, copy the saves and BIOS directories to a computer to guard against mistakes. After the reset, follow a structured setup guide to reapply essential options with guardrails for safe settings and firmware choices. If the crash pattern returns after a clean state, suspect the SD card and run integrity tests or swap media to confirm whether storage is the true root cause.

For misreported battery percentages or sudden shutdown at mid charge, avoid resets and calibrate the pack first, since a reset rarely changes power sensing behavior. A clean calibration will stabilize readings and prevent unnecessary troubleshooting. If unexpected power loss continues after calibration and multiple reboots, test with another SD card and review logs if available. When the device refuses to power on at all, run through a diagnostic flow that prioritizes quick checks before any resets. Only after those steps fail should you consider a factory reset as part of a broader recovery plan that includes reinstalling the operating environment.

Frequently asked questions

Will a soft reset delete my game saves?

No. A soft reset restarts processes and leaves files on the SD card untouched. Saves, BIOS files, and content remain in place. After restarting, reopen your game and confirm new progress is saved correctly before continuing.

When should I choose a factory reset?

Choose a factory reset when crashes persist after multiple reboots, when the launcher fails to load, or after configuration changes that cause repeated errors. Back up saves and BIOS files first to avoid accidental data loss.

Do I need to reinstall Onion OS after a factory reset?

Often you will need to reapply Onion OS options or reinstall components if they were reset. Plan to restore your configuration, confirm BIOS paths, and test a few cores to ensure performance and inputs match your prior setup.

Can a bad SD card cause issues after any reset?

Yes. An unhealthy SD card can recreate crashes, stutter, or corrupt files even after a clean reset. Test card integrity or try a known good card if problems return quickly following either a soft or factory reset.

Is there a risk of bricking during a factory reset?

The risk is low when instructions are followed, but power loss or file corruption can interrupt the process. Keep battery charge healthy, use a reliable SD card, and do not interrupt the device during the reset procedure.
