Clear Caches and Rebuild Thumbnails in Onion OS

By Jordan Kelly • Published March 2, 2026

When menus feel sticky or artwork goes missing, a focused maintenance pass can restore Onion OS responsiveness. This guide shows how to clear caches and rebuild thumbnails without touching saves or ROMs, using a structured plan that reduces risk. We assume a Miyoo-style handheld running Onion OS, an SD card reader, and access to a Windows, macOS, or Linux computer for file operations. You will learn safe deletion boundaries, quick verification tactics, and optimizations you can revisit later as your library grows.

Key takeaways

  • Back up saves before clearing caches to prevent accidental data loss.
  • Delete only temporary folders, never ROM or save directories in Onion OS.
  • Rebuild thumbnails after cleanup so menus repopulate artwork correctly.
  • Validate with a small playlist first to confirm performance gains.
  • Schedule light maintenance to avoid ballooning cache and stale images.

Plan the work

Start by defining scope so you only clear caches tied to performance and thumbnail generation while preserving saves. List targets like temporary files, image caches, and stale playlists. Exclude ROMs, BIOS files, and save data, since removing those creates repair work and possible loss of progress. Create a checkpoint plan with three stops: pre-backup, cleanup window, and validation. A simple rule is to never delete folders that hold irreplaceable content and to always snapshot saves first. This planning step reduces risk and shortens recovery time if a mistake occurs, because you can roll back quickly and resume play with minimal downtime.

Assess symptoms to ensure clearing caches will address the actual problem. If menus lag, thumbnails fail to appear, or artwork flickers when scrolling, cache bloat and stale indexes are likely contributors. However, if the device shows a black screen or fails to boot, start with a broader diagnostic path instead of cache cleanup. For that scenario, follow guidance such as the structured diagnostic path with safe resets described in the related black screen resource to isolate power, SD, or firmware causes. Matching the action to the symptom keeps efforts focused and prevents unnecessary file operations that might mask a deeper fault.

Establish success criteria and time-box your work so you know when to stop. Define targets like menu scrolls without stutter, thumbnails loading within a second on the first screen, and consistent images across a small test playlist. Plan to measure before and after by timing a scroll across a known platform list or watching for hitching during the first load. If the improvements are marginal, log what changed and escalate to a wider rebuild or SD integrity check. Setting boundaries and metrics ensures the cleanup is both efficient and repeatable, and it also builds confidence for future maintenance cycles.

Prepare environment

Create a clean working environment before touching files to avoid compounding errors. Power off the handheld fully, remove the SD card, and use a reliable reader to mount it read write on your computer. Prepare a backup folder on your desktop and copy saves, save states, and configuration files. A practical rule is to mirror directories containing saves or states and leave ROM folders alone to reduce copy time. Consider verifying the SD card with your operating system’s disk tools. If minor corruption appears, repair it first to avoid cache rebuilds failing or thumbnail databases writing incomplete entries.

Map where caches and artwork live so you can target deletions precisely. Temporary files and old thumbnails often accumulate in system-specific cache directories or image folders associated with the frontend and emulators. On many setups, thumbnail images live alongside ROM directories in images or media subfolders, while emulator caches sit under configuration paths. If any path is unclear, sort directories by size and date to spot outliers that grew recently. The tradeoff is that sorting takes time, but it prevents deleting useful files. The goal is to remove expendable content only, keeping core assets intact for reliable rebuilds.

Decide on your rebuild method next because it influences what you delete now. If you will regenerate thumbnails through your frontend or RetroArch playlists, you can safely clear existing artwork folders to force a fresh pull. If you maintain custom artwork, archive it separately so it does not get overwritten during the rebuild. Choose the smallest viable scope at first, like a single platform, to validate your method before scaling up. Starting small limits exposure to unexpected incompatibilities, such as mismatched ROM names or incorrect playlist paths, which can waste time if you attempt a full library rebuild immediately.

Execute steps

Use a precise sequence to minimize risk and confirm progress after each operation. The core flow is to remove temporary caches, wipe stale artwork for the pilot platform, then trigger a rebuild from your frontend or RetroArch playlists. Before starting, confirm your backup exists and the SD is writable. When removing files, keep deletions reversible by moving items to a temporary folder on your computer first. That way, you can restore a subset if the frontend depends on a specific structure. This approach trades a little disk space for flexibility while you test whether thumbnails repopulate and scrolling improves.

If you prefer automating cleanup on macOS or Linux, a small script can target common cache patterns on a mounted SD. The intent is to delete temporary folders named cache or tmp within known app directories while leaving saves untouched. Run the script only after verifying the mount path and making a backup. The expected outcome is reclaimed space and a consistent starting point for thumbnail rebuilding on your pilot platform.

After cleanup, trigger artwork regeneration in your chosen frontend or in RetroArch by rescanning your pilot platform. Ensure your playlist paths match the ROM directory and the artwork naming convention. If thumbnails are sourced from local images, place new art files alongside the ROMs using consistent names. For downloaded assets, verify network connectivity and allow time for the first pull, which may be slower. A small table of options below clarifies which action to choose based on your current state. Validate results

Confirm that thumbnails repopulated and menus feel responsive before expanding scope. Start with your pilot platform and scroll a full page. Watch for stutter, blank tiles, or delayed image loads. Measure a small sample, such as initial thumbnails appearing within about a second and steady frame pacing while scrolling. If results look clean, open a second platform with similar library size to cross-check. This staged validation catches naming conflicts or path mismatches early. If you spot failures, revert the pilot images from your temporary copy and adjust playlist mapping before retrying, which avoids compounding errors across the entire library.

Verify the integrity of paths and playlists when artwork remains incomplete. Open your frontend’s playlist editor or configuration and ensure the directory fields align with the actual ROM structure. A common edge case arises when ROMs were reorganized or renamed, leaving the database pointing to outdated locations. The fix is to rescan with the updated directory and consistent naming conventions. If custom artwork is part of your workflow, confirm the file extensions match what the frontend expects. This precision prevents repeated rescans that produce no change because the underlying references are still broken.

Evaluate storage and card health to ensure improvements persist. If the SD card is nearly full, thumbnail regeneration will compete for space, slowing writes and risking incomplete caches. Freeing even a modest percentage of capacity can restore smooth writes. Run a basic filesystem check and consider replacing aging or unreliable cards if errors recur during scans. Although card swaps add cost, they eliminate a frequent source of corruption that cache cleanup alone cannot solve. Prioritizing media health ensures the rebuilt thumbnails and cache structures remain stable through future updates and library changes.

Troubleshoot and optimize

When thumbnails still fail to appear, inspect naming and extensions first because mismatches are the most common cause. Compare a working entry against a missing one to spot differences in case sensitivity or file type. As a fallback, try rebuilding a single title’s thumbnail manually to see if the pipeline works at all. If manual success contrasts with automated failure, the playlist or directory mapping needs correction. For broader boot or UI problems, review a structured diagnostic resource that outlines safe resets and recovery tactics, which can clear intervening faults that interrupt artwork loading or cache operations on startup.

If the device boots but stalls at the logo, cache tasks will not run reliably. Resolve the startup state before attempting rebuilds to avoid wasted effort. A practical approach is to follow a guide focused on diagnosing why the system gets stuck and executing targeted recovery steps. Once stable, repeat the pilot rebuild on a small platform to verify that the environment no longer interferes. This two-phase approach trades speed for certainty, but it prevents repeating rebuilds that fail silently due to unresolved boot conditions lurking beneath normal operations.

Optimize for longevity by scheduling light maintenance rather than heavy cleanups. Plan a quarterly pass to remove obvious temp folders, refresh a representative platform, and verify scrolling performance. Keep a short log describing what you removed and which settings you used for playlist scans. Over time, this record becomes a playbook you can apply quickly after updates or library changes. If updates introduce instability, consider conservative rollback procedures that preserve data while restoring a working baseline, then repeat the validation process. This steady cadence prevents cache bloat and keeps thumbnails fresh without consuming entire evenings of trial and error.

Quick start checklist

  1. Power down and back up: copy saves and states to a safe folder.
  2. Mount the SD card: use a reliable reader and confirm write access.
  3. Delete cache folders: remove temp and cache directories only, not ROMs.
  4. Clear pilot images: wipe artwork for one platform to force a rebuild.
  5. Rescan playlists: rebuild thumbnails in your frontend or RetroArch.
  6. Verify performance: scroll lists and check for missing or slow thumbnails.
