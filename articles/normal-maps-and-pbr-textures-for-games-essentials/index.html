Normal Maps and PBR Textures for Games: Essentials

By Sarah Bennett • Published January 13, 2026

Normal maps and PBR textures work as a system that reshapes light without adding geometry. This article orients artists and modders moving between older shaders and modern engines by explaining how surface direction, roughness, and metal behavior interact. The focus is practical decision making, like when to choose PNG or DDS, how to keep color space consistent, and what to verify when a model looks strangely glossy. By drawing lines from fixed function tricks to today’s physically based rendering, we will frame reliable habits that survive tool changes. You will leave with vocabulary that maps directly to engine settings and export options, plus a checklist mindset for diagnosing confusing highlights or flat shading on assets you import.

Key takeaways

  • Normal map orientation and color space decide believable lighting immediately.
  • Pack roughness, metallic, and AO to cut memory without harming fidelity.
  • Choose PNG or DDS based on engine support and streaming needs.
  • Verify tangent basis, then rebake or reimport before repainting details.
  • Test PBR materials under varied lights to catch edge case artifacts.

Concepts and definitions

Normal mapping simulates fine surface detail by altering per pixel lighting using a tangent space vector. The RGB channels encode the X, Y, and Z components of the surface normal, commonly in DirectX orientation where green is positive up. A simple rule is to expect a mostly bluish look because Z leans toward the surface normal. A common trap appears when a normal appears inverted, producing dents instead of bumps; this often stems from mismatched Y channel conventions. Understanding why this happens matters because engines expect consistent tangent space and color space. If a normal map is authored in linear color but imported as sRGB, the math skews, softening edges. Recognizing this separation between storage and interpretation prevents silent degradation when assets move across tools.

PBR materials describe how light reflects using a few standardized textures that align with physical intuition. The albedo or base color omits baked lighting and sits in sRGB, while roughness controls microfacet spread in linear space. Metallic marks whether a surface behaves like metal, passing base color into specular and muting diffuse response. Ambient occlusion encodes crevice darkening and should multiply lighting carefully to avoid double shadowing. The why behind these divisions is energy conservation and separation of concerns, so lighting stays consistent under different scenes. A typical rule of thumb keeps metal values near 0 or 1, with transitional edges rather than noisy mid grays. That practice stabilizes reflections and avoids confusing mid tone metals that flicker after compression.

Choosing texture formats decides performance and fidelity before any shader executes. Lossless PNG preserves albedo edges for crisp UI or decals, while DDS BC5 keeps two channel normal data clean with block compression. Roughness and metallic often share channels inside a single packed texture to save memory and bandwidth. This packing works because each input is a scalar that survives channel isolation. The tradeoff is workflow complexity, since debugging becomes harder when the red channel is roughness and green is metallic. A practical compromise writes ambient occlusion to blue and leaves alpha for masks or transmission, depending on the engine. Knowing how your target runtime samples and filters each channel lets you avoid over compression that erodes delicate highlights.

Frameworks and decision lenses

A simple decision lens asks which signals must remain lossless and which can survive compression. Preserve albedo edges and UI glyphs with PNG to avoid ringing, but prefer BC7 for complex color when streaming to GPUs. Use BC5 for normals by storing X and Y while reconstructing Z in shader, which balances quality and size for most assets. Keep roughness and metallic in a single BC4 or packed BC7 when the engine supports it to reduce memory fetches. The tradeoff arises on mobile or legacy hardware where BC formats differ, so fallback to ETC2 or ASTC with similar thinking. The why is straightforward: different maps tolerate artifacts differently, and choosing per map avoids paying for invisible precision.

A verification framework prevents hours of repainting that hide import mistakes. First, prove orientation by rendering a neutral test sphere under a single moving light and checking whether a diagonal ridge pops outward. Second, confirm color space by toggling sRGB import flags and looking for contrast loss in the normal’s fine features. Third, test the tangent basis by rotating the model; if seams flip under rotation, rebake with matched tangents or use mesh tangents at import. This order matters because it isolates fault domains. The risk is misattribution, where artists retouch maps to fix a shader or import flag. Locking down these steps reduces churn and makes comparisons between tools trustworthy.

A packing policy guides texture atlases and installer sizes for mods that ship broadly. Start with a goal like fitting a character’s materials within two textures at 2K, one for color and one for packed scalar inputs. Place roughness in red, metallic in green, ambient occlusion in blue, and leave alpha for a mask like clearcoat or cavity. The benefit is fewer materials and draw calls, yet the tradeoff appears when future features need more channels. To handle change, reserve alpha judiciously and document the meaning of each channel in a short manifest. That note travels with the asset and avoids decoding mistakes during repacks or format conversions later.

Examples and short scenarios

A remaster of a mid 2000s asset reveals how small assumptions break modern lighting. The original game used per vertex lighting with baked highlights in the diffuse, so the albedo must be repainted to remove fake shine. A fresh tangent space normal is baked from the high poly, stored in BC5, and Z reconstructed in shader to cut size. Roughness starts near 0.1 on chrome accents and 0.6 on leather, then adjusted by measured references. The team discovers inverted Y when dents appear instead of screws, fixed by flipping the green channel at import. The why is lineage: legacy assets hid shading inside color, while PBR expects clean separation that respects energy conservation.

A mod pack targets both PC and handheld hardware, forcing careful texture choices. On desktop, BC7 albedo and BC5 normals keep quality high, while ASTC 6x6 serves mobile builds with minimal visual drift. To save memory, roughness, metallic, and ambient occlusion are packed into one texture sampled once per material. A tradeoff appears when ambient occlusion sharpness suffers under compression, so the team sharpens AO using a mild contrast curve while keeping values physically plausible. Installation size drops measurably, and streaming stutter fades because texture fetch count falls. The scenario shows how packing and format parity preserve look while reducing bandwidth across divergent devices.

A stylized game wants painterly strokes without violating physical intuition. Artists author albedo with hand painted gradients but keep metallic binary so metals read clean in reflections. Roughness becomes the main style lever, with directional variation added through a noise mask multiplied into the roughness channel. Normal maps stay subtle to avoid glitter under high specular, and their amplitude is clamped during bake. A limitation appears if the art style needs rim lighting; the team implements it as a post effect rather than baking into albedo. This preserves the core PBR response so the style layer can be tuned per scene without reauthoring every texture.

Pitfalls, limitations, and edge cases

Mismatched tangent space between baker and engine creates seams that shift as the camera rotates. The fix is to bake using the same tangent basis as the runtime or import mesh tangents instead of letting the engine recompute. A rule of thumb is to test with a moving point light and a rotating model; if a seam crosses a UV border and flips, tangent disagreement is likely. The tradeoff is dependency on tooling that may not export the exact basis, so document the bake settings with your asset. The why is math alignment: lighting derivatives assume a specific frame, and when that frame differs, pixel normals diverge from expected directions.

Color space mistakes silently flatten detail or cause metallics to misbehave. Normal, roughness, metallic, and occlusion should be imported as linear, while albedo remains sRGB. If a packed texture is flagged sRGB, roughness shifts, exaggerating or muting highlights unpredictably. A quick diagnostic is to toggle sRGB on the packed texture and compare specular width; large changes reveal a flag issue. The tradeoff is that some engines apply defaults per slot, so overrides get lost during reimports. Lock the setting in version control and include a note in README files accompanying the texture folder to ensure consistent behavior during repacks.

Overaggressive compression can create banding in roughness that flickers in motion. BC1 and JPEG introduce ringing and block edges that read as physical changes in microfacets. Prefer BC7 or ASTC for scalar maps when available, or pre filter roughness with blue noise dithering to diffuse quantization. The limitation is added complexity and slightly higher cost at import or bake time. Another edge case appears with anisotropy or clearcoat extensions, which need extra channels or specialized formats. Plan ahead by reserving alpha or a secondary mask texture when your project roadmap includes those features to avoid breaking existing packing conventions later.

Looking ahead

The near term win is building a small verification scene that exercises normal maps and PBR inputs under varied lights. Include a turntable, a neutral sky, and a few reference spheres for chrome, plastic, and painted metal. This controlled setup lets you spot inverted Y, wrong color space, and roughness banding within a minute. The tradeoff is time spent assembling the scene, but it pays back with faster reviews and fewer ambiguous screenshots. Making this scene part of asset onboarding ensures consistency across contributors who use different tools or exporters.

Documentation becomes the long term guardrail as assets travel through archives and installers. Write a short manifest that records packing conventions, channel meanings, tangent basis, and color space flags. Keep it beside your textures so anyone repacking for a different platform can preserve intent. A single paragraph can prevent accidental sRGB on packed maps or a flipped normal channel during batch conversion. When distributing mods, pair that manifest with installer notes that state expected engine versions and known renderer limitations to reduce support churn.

Frequently asked questions

How do I tell if my normal map is inverted?

Light a test sphere with a diagonal light and look for bumps turning into dents. Flip the green channel if highlights appear inside grooves. If the issue persists, check the engine’s tangent basis and import orientation settings.

Should roughness and metallic be in sRGB or linear?

Keep roughness and metallic in linear space. Only albedo or base color belongs in sRGB. If a packed texture is flagged sRGB, you will see specular width change dramatically when toggling the flag.

What format is best for normal maps?

BC5 is a strong default on desktop and consoles because it stores X and Y cleanly and reconstructs Z in shader. On mobile, use ASTC with a suitable block size to minimize artifacts while keeping memory reasonable.

Is ambient occlusion required in a PBR workflow?

No, but it helps preserve crevice contrast. Use AO sparingly to avoid double shadowing. If your lighting pipeline already includes screen space occlusion, reduce AO strength to prevent overly dark cavities.

Can I paint stylized highlights into albedo?

Avoid painting specular highlights into albedo. Keep style in roughness variation or post effects so lighting remains physically consistent. Painted highlights will not respond to scene lights, causing inconsistent results.
