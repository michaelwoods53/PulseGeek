Evaluation criteria and how to weigh them

Start with a consistent test frame so 40 fps means identical workload across Steam Deck and ROG Ally. Weight TDP and efficiency next because they directly shape watt-hours per minute at 40 fps. Thermals and ergonomics should influence weighting because heat and grip fatigue shorten useful playtime before the battery actually empties. A device that stays cooler at 40 fps may allow longer continuous sessions with less throttling. Measure comfort by the point when palms feel sweaty or fan tone becomes distracting. Higher fan speed can preserve performance yet costs a bit more power and noise. If your travel setup involves quiet environments like flights, weigh acoustics higher. Conversely, if you mostly play in lounges with ambient noise, prioritize lower TDP stability and button feel. This criterion reminds you that endurance is not just hours on paper but hours you want to keep playing.

Frequently asked questions

Why choose 40 fps instead of 30 or 60?

Forty fps often balances responsiveness and efficiency. It improves input feel over 30 while requiring less power and heat than 60, which extends battery life and keeps fan noise down in travel settings.

How should brightness be matched for fair comparisons?

Use similar nit levels measured by a meter or use a consistent percentage only if both panels scale linearly. Matching visual luminance avoids skewed results from brighter screens drawing more power.

Does lowering resolution save more power than a TDP limit?

It depends on the workload. Reducing GPU-bound settings like resolution often cuts power significantly, but if CPU spikes cause dips, a modest TDP increase may stabilize frames with similar battery impact.

What power bank capacity is practical for these devices?

Choose a bank that meets voltage and current requirements and targets at least one full recharge. Higher capacity helps on long trips, but weight and airline rules can limit what is comfortable to carry.

Will undervolting always extend battery life?

Undervolting can reduce power draw at a given performance level, but headroom and stability vary by unit. Test in short sessions and back off if you notice crashes or inconsistent frame pacing.
