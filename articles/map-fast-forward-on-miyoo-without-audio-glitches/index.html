Map Fast Forward on Miyoo Without Audio Glitches

By Logan Ward • Published January 7, 2026

Yes, you can map fast forward on Miyoo while avoiding audio glitches by using the right hotkey mode, enabling audio sync, and applying per core overrides when needed. The cleanest setup pairs a toggle for fast forward with VSync on and muted audio during speedup to prevent pops. This guide explains the short answer and then details when each option applies, how to implement it safely, and what to try if cores behave differently. Along the way, you will validate the mapping, confirm timing stability, and decide whether to keep fast forward audible or silent depending on game type.

Key takeaways

  • Use a toggle fast forward with audio mute to avoid pops.
  • Keep VSync and audio sync on for steady pacing during speedup.
  • Set per core overrides if one emulator resamples too aggressively.
  • Test with a scrolling scene and a steady tone to expose glitches.
  • Map hotkeys that avoid conflicts with menu and save state actions.

Short answer and nuance

The fastest path is mapping fast forward as a toggle instead of a hold, then keeping both VSync and audio sync enabled so timing remains predictable. A toggle reduces constant hotkey scanning, which can spike input latency or cause minor crackles on some Miyoo builds. For audio, enabling the option to mute during fast forward removes resampling artifacts entirely, which many players prefer during long grinds. The downside is you cannot hear progress sounds while sped up, which might matter for rhythm cues or timed inputs. If you want audio during speedup, keep sync enabled and limit the fast forward ratio so the resampler is not pushed beyond a stable range.

Choosing toggle versus hold depends on how often you need controlled bursts versus sustained acceleration. Toggle suits extended fast travel or grinding because it avoids fatigue and event-driven pops when pressing and releasing the hotkey repeatedly. Hold suits short skips over dialog but can provoke tiny glitches if the audio buffer is tight, especially when the device is under load from shaders or scaling. A useful compromise is mapping both actions to different buttons: a toggle for long sections and a hold for quick taps. If you map both, ensure priority is clear so you do not trigger conflicting states in RetroArch’s hotkey system.

Audio sync should remain on for most setups because it keeps frame pacing aligned with the audio clock, which prevents stutter while speeding playback. When sync is off, video can run free and the mixer may chase it, which often creates pops during rapid time dilation. The exception is when a core’s audio implementation already buffers deeply and stays stable at 2x; in that case, audible fast forward can work with sync on and no mute. If you hear intermittent crackles under load, enabling mute during fast forward usually resolves it instantly. This is why a toggle plus mute combination offers the most consistent result across varied cores.

When it applies vs when it does not

This setup excels on RetroArch cores that respect VSync and maintain a modest audio buffer, such as common 8 and 16 bit systems on Miyoo hardware. When you keep audio sync enabled and mute during fast forwarding, the device avoids resampling strain while the GPU and display pace frames predictably. It is especially effective with scrolling platformers and top down RPGs where visual continuity matters more than sound cues. The tradeoff is silence during the accelerated period. If a game telegraphs events through audio, you may miss cues. In those cases, lower the speedup factor or disable mute, then retest for stability.

There are edge cases where hold fast forward is smoother, particularly in dialog heavy scenes that you skip in short bursts. Brief taps reduce time under acceleration and the mixer rarely needs to resample aggressively. However, holding the button through busy scenes can still cause small ticks if the system is also upscaling or running a heavier shader. Disabling shaders during fast forward can help but increases visual differences between normal and accelerated play. When testing, watch frame timing overlays if available and listen for faint crackles on transitions, which often indicate the buffer is too small or speedup is too high.

Some cores implement their own frame throttling or speed control that conflicts with RetroArch’s fast forward behavior. In those cases, letting the core manage speedup may reduce audio artifacts more than frontend controls. If a core adds latency during speedup, consider a per core override that limits fastforward ratio to a conservative value and leaves audio sync on. Validate the setup with a repeatable scene, such as a side scroller with a steady camera pan. Listen for crackles when enabling and disabling fast forward and watch for jitter on HUD elements that should move smoothly across the screen.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use toggle or hold for fast forward on Miyoo?

Use toggle for long stretches like traversal or grinding. It reduces button fatigue and avoids repeated buffer pressure. Use hold for short skips. If both are mapped, ensure no conflicts with other hotkeys.

Does audio sync need to stay on during fast forward?

Yes for most setups. Audio sync keeps pacing steady and prevents pops when speed changes. If a core stays clean without it, you can experiment, but keep VSync enabled to avoid jitter or tearing.

How do I stop crackles when returning to normal speed?

Lower the fastforward ratio or enable mute during speedup. If the issue persists, create a per core override with a smaller ratio and slightly higher audio buffer or timing skew for that emulator.

Can I keep audio audible while fast forwarding?

Yes, but cap the ratio to a moderate value to limit resampling stress. Keep audio sync on and test with a steady tone. If artifacts appear, lower the ratio or switch to muting during speedup.

Why does one core glitch while another is clean?

Cores handle timing differently. Some buffer audio more deeply or throttle frames internally. Use per core overrides to tailor the ratio and sync options so the unstable core uses more conservative settings.
