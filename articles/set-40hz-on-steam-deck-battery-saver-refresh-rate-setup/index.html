Set 40Hz on Steam Deck: Battery Saver Refresh Rate Setup

By Tyler Brooks • Published January 26, 2026

Setting a 40Hz refresh rate on Steam Deck is a reliable way to cut battery draw while preserving smooth input response. This how-to explains when 40Hz helps, how to align FPS caps and frame pacing, and where to measure watt savings. You will use the Quick Access Menu in SteamOS, in-game frame limiters when available, and the performance overlay to verify consistent times. Assumptions include a current SteamOS build, familiarity with basic Deck settings, and willingness to test a couple of titles that stress the GPU differently. Bring a USB-C charger only for baseline measurement, then unplug to evaluate impact in real play. The steps apply to the LCD and OLED models, with notes where panel brightness changes the tradeoff.

Key takeaways

  • Set system refresh to 40Hz and match FPS cap to avoid stutter.
  • Use the performance overlay to confirm stable frame times and watts.
  • Prefer per-game profiles so fast titles can stay at 60Hz.
  • Reduce OLED or LCD brightness after 40Hz to amplify savings.
  • Retest in heavy scenes to ensure consistent pacing at the new rate.

Plan the work

Start with a clear goal: reduce power draw by syncing refresh rate and FPS at 40 while keeping motion perception acceptable. The key is matching system refresh with a game-level or system-level frame limit to maintain even frame pacing. For a baseline, measure current watt usage at 60Hz with your usual settings and one demanding scene, then repeat on a simpler title to capture a second point. This establishes a before and after reference that exposes real savings instead of guesswork. There is a tradeoff in motion clarity and latency. Many players accept 40 as a middle ground between 30 and 60, but fast shooters might feel better at 60. Plan per-game profiles so you can keep 60 where needed and use 40 elsewhere.

Translate that goal into testable metrics. Focus on average system power in watts, frame time stability around 25 milliseconds for 40 frames per second, and subjective input feel in your most played genres. Use SteamOS performance overlay levels that show both FPS and power draw so you can spot drift during longer sessions. If a title has an in-game limiter, prefer it to reduce overhead, but note that some limiters add latency or uneven pacing. If the limiter introduces judder, fall back to the Steam overlay FPS cap which is consistent across titles. Expect minimal difference between LCD and OLED for refresh configuration, but brightness will materially change battery usage, so hold brightness constant when comparing runs.

To avoid confusion, outline decision rules before you change anything. Use 40Hz when the GPU cannot hold 60 FPS without large power demand, and when 30 FPS feels sluggish. Keep 60Hz for twitch games where reaction time takes priority. Choose 30Hz only for visual novels or slow strategy when you need maximum runtime and motion cost is minimal. Document your chosen titles and the reasoning, so you can revisit settings after updates. For players who mod, note that shader compilation or overlays can destabilize frame times, so recheck pacing once those tools are active. Planning reduces backtracking later and sets realistic expectations for battery savings at 40 compared with 60.

Prepare environment

Preparation focuses on consistent measurement and stable comparisons. Enable the SteamOS performance overlay at a level that shows FPS, frame time graph, and system power. Pick two test scenes that stress GPU and CPU differently, such as a busy city and a lighter indoor area, then stand still for thirty seconds in each to stabilize readings. Keep Wi‑Fi state unchanged between runs to avoid shifting background power draw, and close downloads in Steam to reduce variability. If you sometimes dock, set this up handheld to reflect worst case airflow and thermals. Preparation also includes ensuring the title is fully updated and shaders are compiled to avoid compilation spikes that look like pacing faults.

Collect baseline stats at 60Hz before any changes. Record FPS range, frame time line straightness, and average watt usage as displayed by the overlay. Make a simple note like 60Hz heavy scene 15 W average, 60Hz light scene 11 W average, along with whether input feels responsive. These numbers create the reference to judge the switch to 40Hz. If your readings fluctuate heavily, extend the sampling to one minute and ignore the first ten seconds to let clocks settle. For games with dynamic resolution scaling, lock it temporarily so resolution does not mask the effect of refresh rate changes. Stability in the test method yields dependable comparisons later.

Before moving on, check related settings that interact with refresh. Screen brightness meaningfully drives power on both LCD and OLED, so pick a fixed level like 150 nits indoors to avoid skewed savings. If you want deeper guidance on pairing brightness with efficiency, see this overview of evergreen battery practices and power profile choices. Also confirm that background downloads are paused and that haptics are at your normal level to keep the baseline realistic. With prep complete, you are ready to adjust the refresh rate and synchronize FPS limits while keeping other variables steady.

Execute steps

Execution is about aligning three elements: the SteamOS refresh rate, the FPS limiter, and the game profile where the setting should persist. You will use the Quick Access Menu to set the system refresh to 40Hz, then either the in-game limiter or the Steam overlay FPS cap to target 40 FPS. Some games offer a 40 FPS option that pairs cleanly with an even frame time graph around 25 milliseconds. Others need the overlay cap for consistent pacing. Decide whether to set a per-game profile so the Deck recalls your choice automatically, which saves time when switching genres. Smooth pacing is the priority, so be ready to test both limiter paths if the first try stutters.

  1. Open the performance menu: Press the Quick Access Menu button, select Performance, then toggle to Advanced View.
  2. Set system refresh to 40Hz: In the refresh selector, choose 40Hz and confirm the screen feels stable.
  3. Match the FPS cap to 40: Prefer an in-game 40 FPS option; otherwise set the Steam overlay cap to 40.
  4. Create a per-game profile: Enable per-game settings so this refresh and FPS pair persists for this title.
  5. Hold brightness constant: Keep your previously chosen brightness to isolate the effect of refresh changes.

Validate results

Validation confirms that 40Hz configuration works as intended without hidden drawbacks. Revisit your two test scenes and watch the frame time graph while moving the camera slowly in a circle. A healthy setup shows tight lines near 25 milliseconds with only minor variance during asset streaming. Compare watt averages to your 60Hz baseline, noting the direction rather than chasing a specific number. Because power depends on scene complexity and background tasks, expect improvement ranges rather than a fixed percentage. If watt draw does not fall or stutter appears, switch between in-game limit and overlay cap to see which delivers steadier pacing. Keep notes so you can build a repeatable habit across new titles.

Next, evaluate subjective feel. At 40Hz, input latency increases slightly compared with 60Hz, but remains responsive in many genres such as action adventures and third-person RPGs. Test with your usual dodges, parries, or aiming adjustments. If motion blur or sample-and-hold makes panning look smeary, reduce in-game motion blur and consider camera sensitivity tweaks. On the OLED model, perceived blur can be lower at similar refresh due to pixel response, but brightness choices still dominate battery outcomes. If you struggle to adapt in a specific shooter, keep that title at 60 while using 40 for everything else. That selective approach maintains comfort without sacrificing savings overall.

Finally, check persistence and edges. Relaunch the game to ensure per-game profiles hold the 40Hz and 40 FPS pairing, then test a demanding combat sequence or dense city area. Watch for brief spikes above 30 milliseconds that repeat predictably. Occasional blips during asset streaming are normal, but repeated hitching suggests the limiter path is suboptimal or background tasks are interfering. Consider pausing cloud sync between levels or ensuring downloads stay off. Validation protects your time by catching mismatches early.

Troubleshoot and optimize

Troubleshooting begins with identifying the failure mode. If you see uneven pacing or microstutter at 40Hz, switch the limiter path first. Many titles prefer the Steam overlay cap for steadier frame times, while some engines behave better with in-game options. If pacing only fails during effects-heavy scenes, consider a modest TDP limit paired with a resolution or FSR 2 setting that reduces GPU spikes without cratering clarity. When the overlay shows watts barely lower than 60Hz, recheck brightness and wireless activity. For a broader protocol on balancing caps with device power features, consult this practical overview of long session efficiency techniques and integrate changes gradually.

When 40 feels sluggish in competitive play, keep a selective approach rather than abandoning the method. Use per-game profiles to store 60Hz for twitch shooters and 40Hz for slower action or exploration. Revisit camera sensitivity and motion blur if panning seems less crisp, and consider turning off film grain which can accentuate perceived jitter. On OLED units, brightness can be reduced further in darker games to compound savings once 40Hz is set. If you also rely on Bluetooth headsets, note that active wireless links add small but measurable draw, so test airplane mode offline to see the full effect of the refresh change before layering other features back in.

For persistent judder that survives limiter swaps, look for background downloads or shader precompilation and wait them out. Verify that shader cache building has completed by watching CPU activity stabilize in the overlay. If issues remain, reset per-game settings and reapply the 40 pair in a clean session. As a last resort, try 45 FPS with 45Hz where available or drop to a stable 30 configuration if the game engine struggles at 40. Once stable, layer small improvements such as moderate TDP limits or reduced brightness in dark scenes to extend runtime further without adding stutter. When a title receives patches, rerun your two-scene test to verify pacing remains clean. Over time you will build a library of per-game configurations that preserve feel where needed and deliver savings everywhere else, which keeps the Deck ready for both long trips and quick sessions.
