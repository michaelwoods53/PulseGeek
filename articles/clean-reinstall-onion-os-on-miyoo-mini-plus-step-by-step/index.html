Clean Reinstall Onion OS on Miyoo Mini Plus

By Logan Ward • Published January 18, 2026

Goal: perform a clean reinstall of Onion OS on a Miyoo Mini Plus without risking corruption or repeat boot problems. Assumptions: a Windows, macOS, or Linux desktop, a known good microSD card, and basic comfort with file management. Prerequisites include a backup destination for saves and BIOS files, a card reader, and stable power for the handheld. This guide uses a plan then execute pattern that reduces errors by sequencing formatting, verification, and first boot in a controlled order. By treating reinstall as a controlled rebuild rather than a quick copy, you minimize hidden state and reduce intermittent issues like stalled boot screens or missing cores.

Key takeaways

  • Back up saves and BIOS before formatting to protect irreplaceable data.
  • Use FAT32 with proper allocation size to avoid silent SD corruption.
  • Verify Onion OS archives by checksum before copying to the card.
  • Boot once with a clean card to let Onion OS build structure.
  • Restore data gradually and retest to isolate problem files.

Plan the work

Start by defining scope and risks so your reinstall does not introduce new faults. A clean reinstall aims to rebuild Onion OS from verified files on a correctly formatted microSD, then restore only known good data. The main failure modes are corrupt archives, flaky cards, and restoring broken configs. List your assets to preserve, such as RetroArch saves, save states, BIOS files, and controller mappings. Plan checkpoints at three stages: after formatting, after copying the base files, and after first boot initialization. This structure isolates defects quickly. For example, if first boot fails before any personal data returns, the cause is likely media or base files, not saves.

Create a backup plan that is straightforward and reversible because rework increases risk. Copy saves from /Saves and /RetroArch/saves, plus BIOS to a separate folder on your computer. If space is tight, prioritize active titles and irreplaceable BIOS unique to your setup. Avoid compressing backups during this phase because archives can hide copy errors and complicate restore tests. Keep a simple manifest text file listing what you backed up and from which folder. This record helps you restore strategically and spot mismatches like missing core configs or renamed directories during validation.

Define your validation criteria early to keep decisions objective. Success should mean the device boots Onion OS to the main menu, launches a core with a test ROM, and writes a new save without error. Add optional but helpful checks, like opening RetroArch, confirming the correct core list appears, and checking remaining free space on the SD. Decide the order for restoring personal data: saves first, then BIOS if required, then additional themes or tweaks. This gradient ensures that if a restore introduces issues, the last action is small and reversible. Mark a fallback, such as rolling back to stock files if Onion OS fails to initialize.

Prepare environment

Use known good media and power to eliminate unstable variables. Prefer a brand new or lightly used A1 or A2 rated microSD from a reputable vendor, since counterfeit cards frequently misreport capacity and fail under write load. If reusing a card, test with a capacity verifier on your OS to confirm integrity. Ensure the Miyoo Mini Plus has enough charge to complete initialization without drops. On desktop, close aggressive antivirus for the copy step to avoid partial writes or quarantined files. Keep the card reader stable and avoid USB hubs that disconnect under power spikes. Stability in this phase prevents subtle file corruption that only surfaces during boot.

Verify your Onion OS archive before use because a single bit error can cascade into boot issues. Download from the maintainers’ official distribution point and calculate a checksum using a standard tool. Compare it against the published hash. If the site rotates versions, keep the archive and the hash together in a versioned folder. When in doubt, redownload and recheck rather than trusting a stale cache. This extra step saves time later by ruling out damaged downloads. Also gather any required BIOS for systems you plan to test, but do not copy them yet. Holding BIOS back until after Onion OS initializes keeps your first boot diagnostic clean.

Format the card intentionally to match Onion OS expectations for directory creation and file limits. FAT32 is broadly compatible for this device, while exFAT often causes issues on small embedded systems. Choose a 32 KB allocation unit when given the option. Perform a full format rather than quick to force bad block remapping or to surface failing media. Label the volume clearly so you recognize it during copy operations. If you are on Windows, consider a utility that supports FAT32 on larger cards beyond stock limits. On macOS and Linux, use system tools that expose allocation size so you can confirm the formatting choices you made.

Execute steps

Now perform the reinstall using a clean sequence that builds Onion OS from scratch and keeps your data separate until the system stabilizes. Start with a freshly formatted card mounted on your desktop. Extract the Onion OS archive and copy the required base folders to the root of the SD exactly as documented by the project. Avoid mixing prior files during this step, even if you think they are harmless. Eject the card safely to flush caches. Insert it into the Miyoo Mini Plus and power on. Allow the device to complete any first boot scripts and structure creation. This first run creates folder scaffolding and verifies the base is functioning before you reintroduce saves.

After the device reaches the Onion OS menu successfully, power down cleanly and move the SD back to the computer. Copy your saves into the appropriate directories, typically /Saves and possibly /RetroArch/saves for core-specific layouts. Avoid adding themes or advanced tweaks yet. Reinsert the card and boot again, then launch a simple ROM through a known stable core to verify that saves load and write back. If everything works, you can proceed with BIOS files for systems that require them. Introduce these assets in small batches so any regression can be tied to a specific group and reversed quickly.

Once core functionality is stable, you can reapply optional customizations carefully. Examples include controller remaps, shaders, and theme packs. Add one customization at a time with a quick functionality check after each. Keep a written list of what you changed and where you placed it. If a customization causes instability, revert the last change and retest. This incremental approach prevents hidden configuration conflicts from stacking. Finally, confirm that remaining free space on the SD is healthy. Leave headroom for save growth and temporary writes during updates. Low free space can mimic corruption by causing partial writes and confusing error messages during operation.

Validate results

Validation proves the reinstall worked and surfaces issues before you invest time restoring everything. First, check that Onion OS boots to the menu without hangs. Note boot time as a baseline. Open RetroArch and confirm core lists populate as expected. Launch a small test ROM that you know is clean. Save in-game, exit to the menu, and relaunch to ensure persistence. If the device freezes or returns to the logo, the issue likely lies in base files or the SD card rather than your personal data. Recording observations here builds a comparison point for any later regression that might follow customizations.

Confirm file integrity and write behavior at the filesystem level using simple checks. After saving in a game, mount the SD on your desktop and check that timestamps changed in the appropriate save folders. Copy a small dummy file to the SD and delete it to confirm write and delete operations work normally. If operations hang, investigate the reader and the OS logs for filesystem errors. Re-run the checksum on the Onion OS archive if you retained it for re-verification. These steps isolate whether the medium or the data is at fault when symptoms appear ambiguous during gameplay or menu navigation.

Test edge components methodically because problems often hide there. If you use BIOS-dependent systems, add one required BIOS set and test a single title for that platform. Verify audio output, input devices, and brightness controls. Observe free space with a target headroom buffer of several hundred megabytes to avoid pressure during updates or shader caching. If a system fails to launch while others are fine, the BIOS set or core configuration is likely misaligned. Rolling back the addition and confirming normal behavior will prevent a long chase in the wrong direction. Maintain your change log to map every test to a specific action.

Troubleshoot and optimize

When failures occur, start with the smallest reversible fix to keep a safe path forward. If the device stalls at the logo or reboots, suspect the SD card or base files. Reformat the card and recopy the verified Onion OS base as a quick isolation step. If problems appear only after restoring saves, move the saves aside and reintroduce them in small batches to find a corrupt file. For recovery without hardware teardown, follow a methodical process like step-by-step boot recovery and safe media preparation described in the related recovery guide. This approach reduces the urge to try many changes at once, which obscures root causes.

For persistent oddities like random app quits or missing cores, reconstruct configuration caches. Delete only nonessential generated folders that Onion OS recreates on boot, then power cycle to rebuild indexes. Confirm your power source is stable and the battery has adequate charge. Unstable power can mimic bad media by interrupting writes. If RetroArch alone fails to open, prioritize core and config checks rather than reinstalling everything. A focused diagnostic path saves time and preserves working components while you target the failing layer, whether it is the frontend, core, or filesystem.

When symptoms cross multiple subsystems, step back to a broader diagnostic flow that separates boot, input, audio, storage, and OS layers. Use a prioritized troubleshooting hub to identify the most likely causes and test them in order. This structured workflow prevents blind alleys and rework. After stability returns, consider optimizations like leaving headroom on the SD, keeping a verified archive and checksum, and documenting a minimal restore kit. These habits make future maintenance faster and safer by turning an emergency reinstall into a predictable routine you can repeat with confidence.

Quick start checklist

  1. Back up saves and BIOS: copy to your computer and list paths in a manifest.
  2. Format the SD FAT32: perform a full format with 32 KB allocation units.
  3. Verify the Onion archive: calculate SHA-256 and compare to the published hash.
  4. Copy base Onion files: extract and copy only required folders to SD root.
  5. Boot once cleanly: let Onion OS build directories and finish first run scripts.
  6. Restore saves in batches: test a known ROM and confirm new save writes.
  7. Add BIOS and tweaks gradually: recheck stability after each change.

Frequently asked questions

Can I reuse an exFAT card for Onion OS on Miyoo Mini Plus?

It is not recommended. Reformat to FAT32 with a 32 KB allocation unit. exFAT often causes instability on embedded devices and can produce boot errors or file write issues during initialization.

Do I need to wipe my saves to fix a bad boot?

No. First test with only the Onion OS base on a freshly formatted card. If it boots, reintroduce saves in small batches to find a corrupt file. This preserves good data and isolates the problem safely.

What if RetroArch will not open after reinstalling Onion OS?

Suspect core or config issues. Rebuild generated caches, confirm free space, and test with a known stable core. If needed, recopy the Onion OS base files to rule out a partial copy or archive corruption.

How can I confirm the Onion OS download is intact?

Compute a SHA-256 checksum and compare it to the published value. On macOS or Linux use shasum. On Windows use Get-FileHash in PowerShell. Mismatches indicate you should redownload and verify again.

Why does first boot take longer after a clean reinstall?

Onion OS creates directories and initializes configuration on the first run. This can add time compared to subsequent boots. Allow the process to finish before power cycling so the structure is built correctly.

Looking ahead

Stability improves when you treat Onion OS maintenance as a routine with clear checkpoints. Keep a verified archive and its checksum, a manifest of your saves and BIOS, and a short list of post-install customizations. Those three assets let you rebuild quickly without guesswork. If future issues appear, revisit the plan and validate stages rather than applying many tweaks at once. With a repeatable reinstall and restore method, you can spend time playing rather than chasing intermittent boot problems, and your Miyoo Mini Plus remains predictable under updates or configuration changes.

You can further streamline future work by keeping a spare card preformatted with base Onion OS and a dated label. Rotate backups after major play sessions to protect new saves. When a new version ships, verify its checksum before switching cards, then run a short functional test with a known ROM. This hot swap approach minimizes downtime if something misbehaves. Document notable differences between versions and keep your change log tidy. Over time, your notes become a reliable map that prevents repeat mistakes and shortens every reinstall to a controlled series of confident steps.
